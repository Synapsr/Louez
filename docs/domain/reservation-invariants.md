# Reservation invariants

What must be true of a reservation, its stock and its money, whoever writes it: the online store, the dashboard, the marketplace, the phone assistant, the API or an agent. The [glossary](glossary.md) defines the words; this page states the rules and says, for each one, whether the code holds it today.

A rule has one of three states:

- **Held**: every writer enforces it, and a test fails when one stops.
- **Partial**: some writers enforce it, or all do and no test protects it. The page names what is missing.
- **Open**: no writer enforces it yet. The page names what is known to break.

A change to a reservation writer keeps every held rule held, and moves a partial or open rule forward or leaves it where it is. It never adds a writer that skips a rule the others follow. When a rule changes state, change it here in the same pull request.

## Stock

**S1. One availability rule per stock kind.** Returnable stock: a unit or a quantity is free on a window when its lifecycle is active, no downtime covers the window, and no reservation in a blocking status overlaps the window widened by the store's turnover buffer, with strict bounds ([ADR 0006](../adr/0006-canonical-availability-semantics.md)). A reservation's window runs to its end date, or to the end a live extension checkout is holding (`reservationAvailabilityEndSql`). Consumable stock has no time dimension: what is free is the quantity on hand less what blocking reservations have not consumed yet. Untracked stock has no limit. The blocking statuses are `confirmed` and `ongoing`, plus `pending` unless the store turned `pendingBlocksAvailability` off (`getBlockingReservationStatuses`); a pending marketplace booking whose hold has expired blocks nothing, of either stock kind (`buildReservationAvailabilityPredicate`). `packages/db` owns the predicates; `reserveInventory` in `packages/api` allocates on them.
On a product whose units are tracked, stock is counted per combination (`allocateStock`, `packages/api/src/services/stock-allocation.ts`). A line asks for units of the combination it chose, of the combination of each unit it holds, or, when it left a choice open, of any combination that matches what it chose on the product's current axes. At an instant, a request fits when the most units the stock can give the other bookings and the request together is the most it can give the other bookings alone, plus all the request asks: the others come first, and what they lack because the store booked them over its stock is not held against the request. A request fits when it fits at every instant of its window. The units held by a line with no exact combination are granted first, one by one, each only if it fits; then the lines, in their order. A line with no exact combination gets the most that fits next to what was granted before it. A checkout line, or a line with an exact combination, is placed whole on the first combination of the deterministic order where what was granted, the lines with no exact combination after it and the other bookings still fit (failing that, where what was granted still fits), and is never moved again. A line that does not fit whole takes the most that fits, a checkout line the most one combination gives: with `onShortfall: "collect"`, that is the `available` of its shortfall.
Whether a unit the request holds is owed to the other bookings is decided apart from that allocation, against the other bookings and the held units only (`heldUnitsOwed`, same file): every unit each line holds, however many the line asks for, in the order of the lines then of their units, is an exact demand of one unit on its own combination, accepted when the other bookings, the held units accepted before it and itself fit at every instant. A unit refused is owed when it would be accepted with no other booking; one refused even then (its combination unknown, empty, or with fewer units than are held on it) is told apart, and cannot occur at a confirmation (slice 4 of [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md)). What the lines ask beyond their units plays no part. Only a change into a status that takes stock asks it (S3, S4).
The rule guarantees that much and no more. It counts quantities per combination, not physical units: two units of one combination, each out for half of the window, serve a request for the whole window on paper, with a swap in the middle. The other bookings that left a choice open, and the request's lines with no exact combination, are placed afresh at each instant: one may be given a unit on Monday and another on Tuesday, and a write can be accepted that leaves no unit such a booking could keep from its start to its end. A cart of several checkout lines that only another placement of an earlier line would fit is refused, as first fit always did; for everything else the rule is exact, with no search and no budget.
_Partial._ Every writer of a booking allocates with `reserveInventory`: the checkout, the dashboard's creation and edit, the extension, and a change into a status that takes stock (`checkConfirmationStock`, behind the store's confirmation and the customer's and payment's). Database tests hold the count of another booking's open choice for the creation, the edit, the checkout and the confirmation. A line booked with no exact combination is served by any unit that matches what it chose, for each of them but the extension, which still asks one combination to cover the whole line; no database test covers the extension's allocation (X1). Besides the limits of the rule, one case escapes it: the availability shown before a write (the storefront, the marketplace, the calendars, through `computeReservedNetOfExcludedUnits`) still counts an open choice on the product alone: it can show a combination free that the write then refuses.

**S2. A write that can raise demand checks capacity under the product locks.** A new booking, a line added, a quantity raised, dates moved, and a change into a blocking status all read the free stock after `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the products, in the transaction that writes.
_Partial._ Returnable stock: every writer checks, with the rule of S1. `updateReservation` does whenever the edit moves what a stock-holding reservation asks for, per product and per size or combination chosen (`compareReservationDemand`). A change into a blocking status also checks, under the same locks, each unit the reservation holds (S4). Required accessories are checked where a customer composes a cart, under the product locks: the checkout and the extension refuse a cart that lacks one. A change into a blocking status confirms the lines as they are, and the store's creation and edit do not ask for them ([Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md), slice 3). Consumable stock is reconciled, and refuses to go below zero, on an edit of a `confirmed` or `ongoing` reservation only: an edit of a `pending` one is not checked against it.

**S3. The store may book over its returnable stock, and only on purpose.** Louez never leaves a renter unable to record what they decided. When stock is short, the store's write is refused with the list of shortfalls (product, quantity asked, quantity free); sent again with `allowOverbooking`, it is written, its activity row records `overbooked` and the shortfalls, the reservation's history marks that row, and the inbox shows it until it is over. At a confirmation, the override covers only the units a line does not hold yet: a unit the reservation holds that the other bookings need refuses, flag or not (S4); the reservation's own unassigned units never make one of its units owed, and are booked over as any shortfall. That holds for a creation, an edit and the store's confirmation of a request or a quote. A key or an agent may send it on a confirmation, after a preview of the shortfalls that is always asked. A customer, a payment, the marketplace and the phone assistant never get that choice: their booking or their confirmation is refused. Consumable stock is physical: no override takes it below zero. The turnover buffer is not stock: this flag never passes a shortfall that only the buffer makes; the store passes it with its own flag, on that change alone (S4).
_Partial._ Creation, edits and the store's confirmation follow it, with the same check (`reserveInventory`, `onShortfall: "collect"`), the same shortfalls, the same trace written in the write's transaction (`services/capacity-verdict.ts`), and the same inbox item, which leaves once a later edit or confirmation finds that the stock fits: every confirmation that checks the stock records its verdict. The store's confirmation answers `RESERVATION_INSUFFICIENT_CAPACITY` with the shortfalls counted without the buffer, and `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` for lines short only with it (`checkStoreConfirmationInventory`); its flag is always confirmed by a key or an agent, and the row records the credential. The dashboard's history marks every verdict row with `overbooked: true`, the store's confirmation as a creation or an edit (`showsOverbookedBadge`, tested). The customer's acceptance and the paid confirmation call `checkExistingReservationInventory`, which takes no override, and refuse as before, naming nothing; `scripts/reservation-writes.test.mjs` (R7) keeps the store's check to the store's status service, and database tests hold each path ([Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md), slice 4). The override books over what other bookings hold of a consumable and have not taken yet, never over what is on hand: a write that would take it below zero is refused with `errors.consumableInsufficientStock`. A confirmation's shortfalls name their line and what it asks of the product's attributes as the check read it (`reservationItemId`, `selectedAttributes`), so its token cannot pass a shortage that moved to another line of the same product, nor one on a line whose choice the product's configuration widened since.
Since slice 5b of [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md), `overbooked` means the same for every writer: the creation, the edit and the confirmation tell the shortfalls apart through one function (`splitShortfallsByTurnoverBuffer`), their capacity verdict counts only what is short without the buffer, and a line short only with it, when a flag of the write let it through, is recorded apart (`turnoverBufferOverridden`, the lines, the buffer's minutes, the credential) and listed nowhere. An edit that only shortens a reservation confirmed past the buffer, or its neighbour, writes `overbooked: false`.
Still open: the creation and the edit still refuse a shortfall only the buffer makes as short stock, and their `allowOverbooking` still passes it, against this rule's last sentence; they now record it as passed past the buffer, not as an overbooking. The creation has no buffer flag, and the edit's `overrideTurnoverBuffer` leaves the buffer out of its whole check. Their dialogs do not tell the two apart (section 5 there).

**S4. A unit serves one reservation at a time.** A unit assigned to a line is never assigned to another line whose reservation blocks the same window. This one is not for the store to override: two customers cannot leave with the same bike. Only a conflict that comes from the turnover buffer alone can be passed, with `overrideTurnoverBuffer`.
_Partial._ Where the store gives a unit or moves a reservation's dates, a hard conflict is refused, and a conflict that comes from the turnover buffer alone is refused unless the write carries `overrideTurnoverBuffer`: an assignment (`assignUnitsToReservationItem`), a move to another unit from the product page (`reassignReservationItemUnit`), and an edit that changes the dates of a reservation keeping its units (`updateReservation`). With the flag, that write's stock check also leaves the buffer out. So does the store's confirmation, for the lines that fit the stock only without the buffer: refused with `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` and the lines, it passes them with `overrideTurnoverBuffer`, which never covers a line short on the stock itself, and its activity row records `turnoverBufferOverridden`, the lines, the buffer's minutes and the credential; a key or an agent sending it is always asked to confirm a preview of the lines ([Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md), slice 5). A unit can be assigned to a reservation that does not block yet (a quote, or a request when requests do not block), then to a confirmed one: a change of the first into a status that takes stock checks each unit it holds by id, under the product locks, against the other blocking bookings of its window and against the unit's lifecycle and downtimes (`checkConfirmationStock`, behind the store's and the customer's and payment's entries), and is refused while one cannot serve it, `allowOverbooking` or not. It is refused the same way for a held unit no other booking holds but that the other bookings need, counted without the buffer (`heldUnitsOwed`, S1): one that left a choice open, or one not given its units yet, has no room left once the unit is taken. That is decided against the other bookings and the reservation's held units only, never against where its line is placed, and covers every unit a line holds, also past what it asks for. The conflict carries `reason: "owed_to_other_bookings"`, and the store removes or replaces the unit before the flag can book over what is still short. The allocation of the lines is the one of every other writer; the confirmation adds this refusal, for every caller. The store's status change answers `RESERVATION_UNITS_UNAVAILABLE` with the units; the customer's quote acceptance and a paid checkout's confirmation answer as for stock that is gone, and name no unit. The customer's and the payment's never carry `overrideTurnoverBuffer` (their check has no place for it); the store's change carries it only for the buffer-only shortfalls of its lines, never for a held unit's conflict. A conflict that comes from the turnover buffer alone does not refuse the change: the change's activity row names those units (`turnoverBufferOnlyUnitIds`), which `reservations.get` returns with the activity; the dashboard's timeline does not show them, and the inbox does not list them (its `unit_conflict` compares a held unit with the unit's retirement and downtimes, never with another booking). The change's stock check still applies the buffer (S1). Database tests hold the three paths, both ways, and the store's path for a unit another booking's open choice needs and for a unit held past its line's quantity that another booking needs, with and without the flag (the first through an agent too); the three paths confirm an exact line holding a unit of another combination when nobody else needs it, and the flag books over a reservation's own unassigned units next to the units it holds. A unit given to a line with no exact combination is also checked against the stock: when another booking's open choice needs it, the assignment answers `errors.insufficientCapacity`. The stock check itself never follows a unit through the window: it counts per combination and places the others afresh at each instant (S1), and says only whether a held unit's combination has room for it next to the others, so this rule, by id, is what keeps one unit to one reservation. No test covers the unit conflicts of an assignment or of a date change.
Still open: an override covers the change that carries it, never a later one (D5 in [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md)). A later change that takes stock checks the buffer again; the store passes it with its own override, and the customer's acceptance and the paid confirmation, which cannot, are refused until the store confirms itself. What stays open is that the assignment and the move to another unit do not yet record their override on their own rows, nor the date edit the units it passed (it records the lines it passed since slice 5b there), and that a buffer-only conflict on a held unit accepted at a confirmation is not named to the store, in the history or in the inbox (section 5 there).

**S5. Consumable stock moves with the status.** A confirmation takes the quantity, a cancellation of a confirmed or ongoing reservation gives it back, a completion keeps it used. `changeReservationStatus` does it; an edit reconciles through `reconcileReservationStock`.
_Partial._ The status helper holds it, with database tests, and so does the dashboard's creation. Nothing tests that the dashboard's edit keeps calling the reconciliation.

**S6. A line blocks stock for the reservation's whole window.** The checkout accepts a period per line and prices each line on its own period, then stores only the reservation's dates: a helmet taken for one day of a four-day booking blocks four days.
_Open._ Either lines keep their own period, or the checkout stops accepting one. To decide with the convergence of the creation paths.

## Money

**M1. The totals are the sum of their parts.** The subtotal is the sum of the lines' totals, plus the insurance when it is not carried as a line. The total is what the tax engine gives for the lines, the delivery fee and the discount, tax included (`calculateTaxBreakdown`): with prices that include tax it is the subtotal plus delivery less the discount; with prices before tax, the tax comes on top. The deposit is never part of it.
_Partial._ `updateReservation` still computes the new totals from a read made before its transaction, and refuses the edit when that read no longer holds under its locks (C2): two edits at once no longer leave the lines of one and the total of the other. It builds its total apart from the pricing the checkout uses (M3).

**M2. A reservation has one currency, written on it when it is created.** `reservations.currency` holds it (`reservationCurrency`, `apps/web/lib/reservations/payment-billing-facts.ts`). Every amount stated, asked or held on that reservation is in it, whatever the store's settings say later. An amount sent against an existing provider object, the capture of a hold or the refund of a charge, is in that object's own currency, as the provider states it: a capture against a hold in another currency than the reservation's is refused, and the store captures it from Stripe.
_Partial._ What asks or holds money on an existing reservation reads the reservation's currency: the customer's payment session, the payment request, the dashboard's deposit hold, the customer's own deposit authorization and the extension quote. A capture is counted in its hold's currency, which Stripe is asked for, and a Stripe refund in its charge's; both are recorded as Stripe answered them. So does what states an amount to someone: the emails and text messages, the outbox payload of a status change (a row queued without the field states the store's, and so does a row queued with it but sent by an instance still running the previous code: the guarantee holds once every instance runs the new code), the contract, the invoice and credit note of a payment with no currency of its own, the marketplace's view of a booking, the inbox, and the dashboard's page and edit form of a reservation. A test fails when one of these reads the store's: the dashboard's hold, capture and refund (`deposit-currency.db.test.ts`), the payment session (`payment-session.db.test.ts`), the payment request (`util.payment-request.test.ts`, `actions.db.test.ts`), the extension quote (`extension-quote.test.ts`), each email that states an amount (`lib/email/send.test.ts`), the messages of a new request (`post-creation-effects.test.ts`), the status-change payload (`status-change-effects.test.ts`) and the marketplace view (`marketplace-booking.db.test.ts`). No test covers the customer's deposit authorization page, the contract, the invoice fallback, nor the dashboard's two pages. Still in the store's current currency, on screen and in read answers only: the dashboard's reservation list, calendar and planning, and its customer page; the list, calendar, planning, upcoming and overdue answers of the API (`services/reservations.ts`, one currency per answer, set in `routers/dashboard/reservation-follow-up.ts`; `reservations.get` gives the reservation's own); the customer account's pages, which format every amount with the store's currency (`useFormatMoney`). Rows created before the column existed hold null and fall back to the store's current currency, as does a stored value that is no currency code: a store that changes currency would restate all of them, until a backfill writes the currency they were made in.

**M3. A price is a fact of the booking.** The unit price, its breakdown, the tax rate, the promotion and the promo code applied when the line was priced are stored on the reservation. Only an edit of that line reprices it, and then with every rule the first pricing used. An edit of something else, or a change of the store's or the product's settings, changes no amount of an existing reservation.
_Open._ An edit reprices through the seasonal engine alone and drops the product's promotion. The totals the dashboard writes are later read back with the store's current tax settings.

**M4. Money has one representation.** An amount is a whole number of the currency's minor units, or a decimal string with exactly the currency's digits, and it is rounded once, where it is computed. A currency without decimals has none anywhere.
_Open._ Prices are JavaScript numbers rounded to two decimals in several places, and Stripe amounts are converted apart: a 50 % promotion on ¥101 is ¥50.50 in Louez and ¥51 at Stripe.

**M5. A provider is asked once for one intent.** A charge, a hold, a capture or a refund is first recorded as an attempt under the reservation's lock, then sent with an idempotency key that is stable for that attempt. A retry after a lost answer finds the same provider object. A second caller finds the attempt and does not call. An attempt whose outcome is not known is never replaced by another.
_Partial._ The extension's checkout and refund follow it, and so does the store's deposit authorization (`apps/web/lib/reservations/deposit-hold.ts`): its attempt stays pending, is asked again with the same key, and is settled by a lookup on the connected account it was sent to or by the Connect webhook. It is closed only on what Stripe says: its PaymentIntent, a refusal of the request, or a complete listing of the PaymentIntents of the customer the attempt stored, made more than 24 hours after its last request, that does not hold it. A timeout, a 500 and a 500 replayed for the key close nothing, and neither does any age. Stripe promises no delay for making the PaymentIntent of a request it answered 500 to, so a closed attempt keeps its row (`failed`, with its reservation, account, amount, currency, card and id) and still reconciles: its PaymentIntent, when Stripe's event about it arrives and it matches the attempt, is recorded on that row, as the reservation's hold when it has none, or with its release owed when it has another. No background lookup reads a closed attempt again: that rests on the event's delivery. A wrong verdict of absence costs at most a second hold for a time, recorded once its event arrives, and released when another hold secures the reservation. An attempt nothing can settle (its customer cannot be listed, it has more PaymentIntents than a lookup reads, its request never reached Stripe) is shown by the dashboard and the inbox (`deposit_unconfirmed`), and the store closes it from the dashboard (`reservations.resolveDepositAttempt`, a session only): Louez looks the attempt up first, when it can, and records the PaymentIntent it finds; else the attempt is closed as above, on the store's word. An authorization the store sees in Stripe that Louez has not recorded for the attempt is the store's to cancel in Stripe (`deposit-hold.db.test.ts`, and a random walk over every event a hold can see, the store's closing included, `deposit-hold.model.db.test.ts`). Found is never absent: a PaymentIntent Louez finds for the attempt that the writer refuses (its amount, currency, card, customer, capture method or reservation are not the attempt's: it was altered at Stripe) closes and replaces nothing while Stripe may hold funds on it, whether the store's closing, a request or the closure a day after found it. The attempt stays pending, an activity line records who found it, when, which PaymentIntent and why it was refused, for that attempt's id, the inbox's `deposit_unconfirmed` names it, and the store is told to cancel it in Stripe (`errors.depositAttempt.needsStripe`; `DEPOSIT_ATTEMPT_NEEDS_STRIPE` from `reservations.resolveDepositAttempt`, with the PaymentIntent and the reason). That line blocks the attempt's closing as absent, under the reservation's lock: `closeAbsentAttempt` is the only closure of an attempt as absent, and the one this guard protects; an attempt also ends when the writer settles its own PaymentIntent (authorized, captured, cancelled, failed). A later lookup that fails, stops short or no longer lists the refused PaymentIntent proves nothing about it, and the closing as absent is made only when its caller brings that PaymentIntent read again by its id on the attempt's account, holding nothing (cancelled, or declined with nothing captured). A read that fails, or that shows it may hold funds, leaves the attempt pending and answers `needsStripe` again. The closing then records that it was read empty, and a new hold may be asked for; the line weighs on its own attempt only, and the row does not name that PaymentIntent, which the activity line keeps (`deposit-hold.db.test.ts`, for an altered amount and for removed reservation metadata, for a closing whose lookup fails or ends on an empty page that says more follow, a request Stripe refuses and the closure a day after, both with a complete listing that no longer shows it, a read by id that fails, and one that reads it cancelled). A captured one never holds nothing, so the request stays open. A request whose lookup tells nothing reads the attempt's row, under the reservation's lock, before it answers: one the webhook recorded meanwhile answers what was recorded. An attempt is claimed by one request at a time, and the claim has an owner: the request writes a random token of its own in `payments.claim_token`, with a lease in `claimed_until` on the database's clock, and another request takes the attempt over only once that lease has run out, under a token of its own. Claiming, renewing, giving back and every check that a request still holds its claim lock the reservation's row first, then the attempt's. The request renews its lease before each Stripe call, for one call at its longest; a renewal or a check that finds another token, or none (taken over, given back, or settled by the webhook, which clears it), has lost the claim. A request that lost it writes nothing of what it read from Stripe, calls Stripe no more, and answers from the attempt's row: in progress while the row names no PaymentIntent, else what was recorded there. Tests cover a renewal racing a takeover, a request whose lease ran out while another took the attempt over in the same second, and two claims of the same second (`deposit-hold.db.test.ts`). A deposit capture asks Stripe for its hold's currency first (M2), then sends a key that is stable for its PaymentIntent and amount and records no attempt: after a lost answer, the same capture asked again, or the webhook, records it. The release of a second authorization is an outbox row (`deposit.holdRelease`), added with the write that records it as owed. The ordinary checkout and payment sessions and the dashboard's Stripe refund send no key and record no attempt, and neither does the customer's own deposit authorization, which opens a new PaymentIntent each time its page asks for one and does not look at a pending store attempt: two holds can then exist, and the second one Louez learns of is recorded with its release owed, and released. A customer's authorization made on a connected account the store leaves before any confirmation or webhook recorded it has no row: the events of an account the store left are believed only for the rows that name it. Deleting an account deletes its stores' payment rows and outbox rows, and with them any release Louez still owed and the row a late event needs. Migration 0100 must be applied before this code serves requests: the app runs migrations at startup, and an instance of this code against the old schema fails its payment reads.

Louez itself creates exactly one PaymentIntent per attempt, under one idempotency key; what the deposit authorization leaves open each starts from a PaymentIntent altered or created by hand in the store's Stripe account:

1. one Louez had not found when its attempt was closed is refused when its event arrives, and only the logs say so;
2. one that read empty at the closure and was confirmed again afterwards is refused the same way;
3. one captured there keeps its attempt open, and Louez does not record that money (a manual deposit payment is capped at the deposit still to collect);
4. a second PaymentIntent given by hand the same reservation and attempt reference as an attempt, with matching amount, currency and card, settles that attempt when Louez finds it, whatever evidence exists about another unmatched PaymentIntent of that attempt, which then stays at Stripe untracked.

**M6. The balance is what is owed less what was received, in the reservation's currency.** A payment in another currency does not reduce it.
_Partial._ The statistics filter by currency (`services/sales-analytics.ts`). So do the payment request, the customer's payment session and the extension's paid check: they count a payment only when it is in the reservation's currency (`getSettledPaymentAmount` and `isRentalPaid` given that currency, `packages/utils/src/util.payment-movement.ts`). Only a Stripe payment states a currency of its own, since older rows recorded by hand kept the column's default, and a refund follows the payment it gives back. Tests cover the rule and its three callers (`util.payment-movement.test.ts`, `util.payment-request.test.ts`, `payment-session.db.test.ts`, `extension-quote.test.ts`). Still summing every payment whatever its currency: the dashboard's paid totals and payment summaries (`reservations-utils.ts`, `[id]/util.payment-refunds.ts`), the remaining amount a recorded payment is checked against (`recordPayment`), the customer account's paid amount and paid state, the API's reservation readers (`services/reservations.ts`) and the cancellation preview (`services/reservation-status.ts`). So the customer account can show as paid a reservation the payment session would still charge.

**M7. No more goes back than came in.** A refund never exceeds what its payment still holds, and a deposit return never exceeds the deposit received less what was already returned or captured. The check and the write are one step under the reservation's lock. A hold, its capture and its release are rows about the same provider intent.
_Partial._ A hold, its capture and its release have one writer (`settleDepositIntent`, `apps/web/lib/reservations/deposit-hold-writes.ts`), for the dashboard, the customer's page, the Connect webhook and the outbox alike. What it writes is a pure function of three facts: the state of the PaymentIntent's hold row, its state at Stripe, and what the reservation references (`util.deposit-hold-transition.ts`, tested pair by pair). A hold row follows Stripe and only moves forward; a PaymentIntent that is over always has its final row, so an older snapshot cannot reopen it; the reservation's deposit changes for the PaymentIntent it references, or takes an authorization or a capture when it holds none. The paid confirmation writes the session's customer, card and `depositStatus` only on a reservation on which no funds are held or were taken: when its `depositStatus` is `authorized` or `captured`, or one of its hold rows is `authorized` or `completed`, read after the reservation's lock, it leaves the three as stored (`paidConfirmationKeepsDeposit`, called from `complete-checkout-payment.ts`; `checkout-status.db.test.ts`). A `pending` reservation the paid confirmation left `card_saved` over a hold before that rule is not repaired ([Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md), section 5). A second authorization is never the reservation's hold: its row stays `authorized` with its release owed (`payments.release_owed_at`) until Stripe confirms the cancellation. The debt is what Louez owes, not its outbox row: the outbox asks Stripe until it passes, the cron gives the debt a new row a day after an abandoned or deleted one, and the inbox shows the debt after 30 minutes (`deposit_release_failed`). A failure Stripe reports for one PaymentIntent tells nobody while another holds the reservation's deposit. A PaymentIntent is written on a reservation only when a row names it, on the account that row names; or its attempt, open or closed, has no PaymentIntent yet and was sent to that account with its amount, currency and card; or it comes from the store's connected account of today: an account the store left writes nothing but on the rows that name it (`deposit-hold.db.test.ts`, `deposit-events.db.test.ts`). A row that names a PaymentIntent is believed for its account only: its amount, currency and card are not compared again, since rows written before this code may hold the column's default currency, and refusing the capture or the release of a recorded hold would leave money unrecorded. Refunds of a Stripe payment are bounded by the payment, which Stripe is asked about before the refund, outside any lock. `returnDeposit` and the recorded payments check their limit before their transaction. The agents' `record_payment` (MCP and the in-app assistant) records a `deposit_return` with no limit at all.

## Status

**T1. One function changes a status, one inserts.** `changeReservationStatus` locks the row, checks the transition, moves consumable stock, dates `billableAt` and `withdrawnAt`, and writes the activity row, under a lock token of the owner entry (C1). `insertReservation` is the application's only insert; the token it returns allows one use, the new reservation's stock taken once. `scripts/reservation-writes.test.mjs` fails on a write in another form ([Data layer, DL-12](../code-review/04-data-layer.md)); it reads syntax, exempts the seeds, and does not see the store-cloning script, the billing-date backfill or dynamic SQL.
_Held_ for application code.

**T2. The transitions are a fixed graph.** `pending` goes to `confirmed`, `rejected` or `cancelled`; `quote` to `confirmed`, `declined` or `cancelled`; `confirmed` to `ongoing` or `cancelled`; `ongoing` to `completed` or `cancelled`. Nothing leaves `completed`, `cancelled`, `rejected` or `declined`, and nothing enters `pending` or `quote` (`packages/db/src/consumable-stock.ts`).
_Held._

**T3. A completed reservation is not edited.** Its lines, dates and totals are what was rented.
_Partial._ `updateReservation` refuses it, before and under its lock. No test covers it, nor a completion that lands during an edit.

**T4. A reservation's number is unique in its store.**
_Open._ The dashboard numbers with a count of the year's reservations plus one, read with no lock; the online store draws a number and checks it before the insert, a race its own comment admits (`apps/web/lib/reservations/reservation-number.ts`). The table has no unique index on store and number.

**T5. A reservation's window is a real interval of the store's time.** The end is after the start, and a local date and time the customer or the store typed is one that exists in the store's time zone.
_Partial._ The extension checks the time zone round trip, nonexistent local times included. `updateReservation` refuses only an end before the start, so it accepts a window of no length.

**T6. Pickup follows the contract's rules.** Validating the contract and signing it are two steps (`services/reservation-contract-validation.ts`); a store that requires a signature refuses a pickup without one (`services/reservation-status.ts`).
_Held_ for the store's status changes.

## Promo codes

**P1. A promo code is used as often as its limit says.** Its validity, its limit and what it may combine with are checked when it is applied, and its use is counted in the booking's transaction.
_Partial._ The evaluation (`packages/api/src/services/promo.ts`) and the counting (`apps/web/lib/reservations/apply-promo-code.ts`) are separate steps. To review with the creation lot.

## Extensions

**X1. An extension holds stock while its checkout is live, and is applied only to the reservation it was quoted for.** A payment that completes against a reservation that changed since the quote, or whose stock is gone, is refunded and not applied (`extension-service.ts`).
_Partial._ The extension service does it; its tests replace the transaction with a mock, and none runs on a database.

## Concurrency

**C1. Locks are taken in one order.** The store row, then products by id, then reservations, then units by id ([Data layer, Lock order](../code-review/04-data-layer.md)). Every status change takes its locks through the owner entry of `@louez/db` (`packages/db/src/reservation-change.ts`), READ COMMITTED, and runs the whole transaction again on a lost deadlock or a product added meanwhile, three runs in all. A change that moves stock or runs a stock check locks the reservation's products, then the reservation (`runReservationChange`); the customer's decline and cancellation, the marketplace's expiry and the failed or abandoned checkout, which move none, lock the reservation alone (`runReservationOnlyChange`); the paid confirmation and the marketplace's cancellation choose from the status they read (`runReservationChangeByStatus`), and start over when they took the reservation alone and find another status under it. The store's own change locks the products for every change, whether it moves stock or not. A path that still takes the reservation first retries a lost deadlock once (`retryOnceOnDeadlock`): the extension and the unit assignment.
_Held_ for status changes: `changeReservationStatus` and the consumable stock helpers run only under a lock token the owner entry issues for the transaction, the store and the reservation, so a status change cannot be written without its locks. What a token allows is what the module recorded when it issued it, never what its fields say, and an owner entry's token stops working when its transaction ends. An `insertReservation` token issued in a transaction the owner entry did not open (the dashboard's creation) is valid there by convention only, and allows one stock taking. A call without the token does not compile, throws at run time, and fails `scripts/reservation-writes.test.mjs` (R6); `packages/db/src/reservation-change.db.test.ts` fails when the entry stops locking the products before the reservation, or accepts another transaction's token, a rewritten or copied token, or an inserted token twice; `packages/api/src/services/marketplace-booking.db.test.ts` fails when cancelling a `pending` marketplace booking takes its products' lock; `apps/web/lib/reservations/status-change-races.db.test.ts` fails when a status writer racing the store's change starts a run over. _Partial_ for the rest: the extension and the unit assignment invert the order and retry, and the dashboard's edit follows it through its own code, with no test that fails when a new path inverts it.

**C2. A write decides on what it reads under its locks.** Whatever it read before them it reads again, or it proves nothing changed, before its first write.
_Partial._ The checkout rechecks stock and required accessories under the product locks, and the store's status changes read everything under theirs. The manual creation decides its stock on what it reads under the product locks: the products, the store's availability rules and the combination each line chose. The checkout still takes those rules from a read made before its locks, and both still write the prices they read before locking. `updateReservation` computes before its locks too, then proves under them that nothing it read has changed (`fingerprintReservationEdit`), or refuses the edit. The recorded payments and deposit returns check their limits before their transaction (M7).

**C3. No effect runs inside a transaction that may run again.** A fee, an email, a notification or a provider call runs after the commit, once.
_Held_ for the paths listed under the lock order: the work each status writer gives the owner entry writes rows only (the store's messages go to the outbox in it), and its fees, messages, Stripe calls, insurance and revalidation run after it returns. Established by reading: no test can see an effect inside a transaction.

## Effects

**E1. A message that must not be lost is a row of the outbox, added in the write's transaction** ([The outbox](../architecture/api-and-agents.md#the-outbox)).
_Partial._ The store's status changes do. The messages of a new booking, and those of a customer's quote acceptance or cancellation, are sent after the commit from memory: a crash between the two loses them. The extension's messages have their own recoverable attempts, outside the outbox.

**E2. A write sent twice with the same idempotency key runs once.**
_Partial._ A procedure built on `storeProcedure` replays a recorded success and refuses a concurrent or unknown outcome (`runOperation`). A key whose call failed runs again, also when the handler threw after its commit; a procedure with a secret output ignores the key. The server actions that are not procedures yet have none.

## Measuring

Each rule about data at rest can be counted with a read-only query: totals against lines, reservations over the stock, holds per reservation, currencies, numbers used twice. Run the count on real data before and after a rule moves to held, to see that the writers stopped producing violations. Keep the figures out of this repository.
