# Reservation invariants

What must be true of a reservation, its stock and its money, whoever writes it: the online store, the dashboard, the marketplace, the phone assistant, the API or an agent. The [glossary](glossary.md) defines the words; this page states the rules and says, for each one, whether the code holds it today.

A rule has one of three states:

- **Held**: every writer enforces it, and a test fails when one stops.
- **Partial**: some writers enforce it, or all do and no test protects it. The page names what is missing.
- **Open**: no writer enforces it yet. The page names what is known to break.

A change to a reservation writer keeps every held rule held, and moves a partial or open rule forward or leaves it where it is. It never adds a writer that skips a rule the others follow. When a rule changes state, change it here in the same pull request.

## Stock

**S1. One availability rule per stock kind.** Returnable stock: a unit or a quantity is free on a window when its lifecycle is active, no downtime covers the window, and no reservation in a blocking status overlaps the window widened by the store's turnover buffer, with strict bounds ([ADR 0006](../adr/0006-canonical-availability-semantics.md)). A reservation's window runs to its end date, or to the end a live extension checkout is holding (`reservationAvailabilityEndSql`). Consumable stock has no time dimension: what is free is the quantity on hand less what blocking reservations have not consumed yet. Untracked stock has no limit. The blocking statuses are `confirmed` and `ongoing`, plus `pending` unless the store turned `pendingBlocksAvailability` off (`getBlockingReservationStatuses`); a pending marketplace booking whose hold has expired blocks nothing, of either stock kind (`buildReservationAvailabilityPredicate`). `packages/db` owns the predicates; `reserveInventory` in `packages/api` allocates on them.
On a product whose units are tracked, stock is counted per combination (`allocateStock`, `packages/api/src/services/stock-allocation.ts`). A line asks for units of the combination it chose, of the combination of each unit it holds, or, when it left a choice open, of any combination that matches what it chose on the product's current axes. At an instant, a request fits when the most units the stock can give the other bookings and the request together is the most it can give the other bookings alone, plus all the request asks: the others come first, and what they lack because the store booked them over its stock is not held against the request. A request fits when it fits at every instant of its window. The units held by a line with no exact combination are granted first, one by one, each only if it fits; then the lines, in their order. A line with no exact combination gets the most that fits next to what was granted before it. A checkout line, or a line with an exact combination, is placed whole on the first combination of the deterministic order where what was granted, the lines with no exact combination after it and the other bookings still fit (failing that, where what was granted still fits), and is never moved again. A line that does not fit whole takes the most that fits, a checkout line the most one combination gives: with `onShortfall: "collect"`, that is the `available` of its shortfall.
Whether a unit the request holds is owed to the other bookings is decided apart from that allocation, against the other bookings and the held units only (`heldUnitsOwed`, same file): every unit each line holds, however many the line asks for, in the order of the lines then of their units, is an exact demand of one unit on its own combination, accepted when the other bookings, the held units accepted before it and itself fit at every instant. A unit refused is owed when it would be accepted with no other booking; one refused even then (its combination unknown, empty, or with fewer units than are held on it) is told apart, and cannot occur at a confirmation (slice 4 of [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md)). What the lines ask beyond their units plays no part. Only a change into a status that takes stock asks it (S3, S4).
The rule guarantees that much and no more. It counts quantities per combination, not physical units: two units of one combination, each out for half of the window, serve a request for the whole window on paper, with a swap in the middle. The other bookings that left a choice open, and the request's lines with no exact combination, are placed afresh at each instant: one may be given a unit on Monday and another on Tuesday, and a write can be accepted that leaves no unit such a booking could keep from its start to its end. A cart of several checkout lines that only another placement of an earlier line would fit is refused, as first fit always did; for everything else the rule is exact, with no search and no budget.
_Partial._ Every writer of a booking allocates with `reserveInventory`: the checkout, the dashboard's creation and edit, the extension, and a change into a status that takes stock (`checkConfirmationStock`, behind the store's confirmation and the customer's and payment's). Database tests hold the count of another booking's open choice for the creation, the edit, the checkout and the confirmation. A line booked with no exact combination is served by any unit that matches what it chose, for each of them but the extension, which still asks one combination to cover the whole line; no database test covers the extension's allocation (X1). Besides the limits of the rule, one case escapes it: the availability shown before a write (the storefront, the marketplace, the calendars, through `computeReservedNetOfExcludedUnits`) still counts an open choice on the product alone: it can show a combination free that the write then refuses.

**S2. A write that can raise demand checks capacity under the product locks.** A new booking, a line added, a quantity raised, dates moved, and a change into a blocking status all read the free stock after `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the products, in the transaction that writes.
_Partial._ Returnable stock: every writer checks, with the rule of S1. `updateReservation` does whenever the edit moves what a stock-holding reservation asks for, per product and per size or combination chosen (`compareReservationDemand`). A change into a blocking status also checks, under the same locks, each unit the reservation holds (S4). Required accessories are checked where a customer composes a cart, under the product locks: the checkout and the extension refuse a cart that lacks one. A change into a blocking status confirms the lines as they are, and the store's creation and edit do not ask for them ([Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md), slice 3). Consumable stock is reconciled, and refuses to go below zero, on an edit of a `confirmed` or `ongoing` reservation only: an edit of a `pending` one is not checked against it.

**S3. The store may book over its returnable stock, and only on purpose.** Louez never leaves a renter unable to record what they decided. When stock is short, the store's write is refused with the list of shortfalls (product, quantity asked, quantity free); sent again with `allowOverbooking`, it is written, its activity row records `overbooked` and the shortfalls, the reservation's history marks that row, and the inbox shows it until it is over. At a confirmation, the override covers only the units a line does not hold yet: a unit the reservation holds that the other bookings need refuses, flag or not (S4); the reservation's own unassigned units never make one of its units owed, and are booked over as any shortfall. That holds for a creation, an edit and the store's confirmation of a request or a quote. A key or an agent may send it on a confirmation, after a preview of the shortfalls that is always asked. A customer, a payment, the marketplace and the phone assistant never get that choice: their booking or their confirmation is refused. Consumable stock is physical: no override takes it below zero. The turnover buffer is not stock: this flag never passes a shortfall that only the buffer makes; the store passes it with its own flag, on that change alone (S4).
_Partial._ Creation, edits and the store's confirmation follow it, with the same check (`reserveInventory`, `onShortfall: "collect"`), the same shortfalls, the same trace written in the write's transaction (`services/capacity-verdict.ts`), and the same inbox item, which leaves once a later edit or confirmation finds that the stock fits: every confirmation that checks the stock records its verdict. The store's confirmation answers `RESERVATION_INSUFFICIENT_CAPACITY` with the shortfalls counted without the buffer, and `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` for lines short only with it (`checkStoreConfirmationInventory`); its flag is always confirmed by a key or an agent, and the row records the credential. The dashboard's history marks every verdict row with `overbooked: true`, the store's confirmation as a creation or an edit (`showsOverbookedBadge`, tested). The customer's acceptance and the paid confirmation call `checkExistingReservationInventory`, which takes no override, and refuse as before, naming nothing; `scripts/reservation-writes.test.mjs` (R7) keeps the store's check to the store's status service, and database tests hold each path ([Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md), slice 4). The override books over what other bookings hold of a consumable and have not taken yet, never over what is on hand: a write that would take it below zero is refused with `errors.consumableInsufficientStock`. A confirmation's shortfalls name their line and what it asks of the product's attributes as the check read it (`reservationItemId`, `selectedAttributes`), so its token cannot pass a shortage that moved to another line of the same product, nor one on a line whose choice the product's configuration widened since.
Since slice 5b of [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md), `overbooked` means the same for every writer: the creation, the edit and the confirmation tell the shortfalls apart through one function (`splitShortfallsByTurnoverBuffer`), their capacity verdict counts only what is short without the buffer, and a line short only with it, when a flag of the write let it through, is recorded apart (`turnoverBufferOverridden`, the lines, the buffer's minutes, the credential) and listed nowhere. An edit that only shortens a reservation confirmed past the buffer, or its neighbour, writes `overbooked: false`.
Still open: the creation and the edit still refuse a shortfall only the buffer makes as short stock, and their `allowOverbooking` still passes it, against this rule's last sentence; they now record it as passed past the buffer, not as an overbooking. The creation has no buffer flag, and the edit's `overrideTurnoverBuffer` leaves the buffer out of its whole check. Their dialogs do not tell the two apart (section 5 there).

**S4. A unit serves one reservation at a time.** A unit assigned to a line is never assigned to another line whose reservation blocks the same window. This one is not for the store to override: two customers cannot leave with the same bike. Only a conflict that comes from the turnover buffer alone can be passed, with `overrideTurnoverBuffer`.
_Partial._ Where the store gives a unit or moves a reservation's dates, a hard conflict is refused, and a conflict that comes from the turnover buffer alone is refused unless the write carries `overrideTurnoverBuffer`: an assignment (`assignUnitsToReservationItem`), a move to another unit from the product page (`reassignReservationItemUnit`), and an edit that changes the dates of a reservation keeping its units (`updateReservation`). With the flag, that write's stock check also leaves the buffer out. So does the store's confirmation, for the lines that fit the stock only without the buffer: refused with `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` and the lines, it passes them with `overrideTurnoverBuffer`, which never covers a line short on the stock itself, and its activity row records `turnoverBufferOverridden`, the lines, the buffer's minutes and the credential; a key or an agent sending it is always asked to confirm a preview of the lines ([Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md), slice 5). A unit can be assigned to a reservation that does not block yet (a quote, or a request when requests do not block), then to a confirmed one: a change of the first into a status that takes stock checks each unit it holds by id, under the product locks, against the other blocking bookings of its window and against the unit's lifecycle and downtimes (`checkConfirmationStock`, behind the store's and the customer's and payment's entries), and is refused while one cannot serve it, `allowOverbooking` or not. It is refused the same way for a held unit no other booking holds but that the other bookings need, counted without the buffer (`heldUnitsOwed`, S1): one that left a choice open, or one not given its units yet, has no room left once the unit is taken. That is decided against the other bookings and the reservation's held units only, never against where its line is placed, and covers every unit a line holds, also past what it asks for. The conflict carries `reason: "owed_to_other_bookings"`, and the store removes or replaces the unit before the flag can book over what is still short. The allocation of the lines is the one of every other writer; the confirmation adds this refusal, for every caller. The store's status change answers `RESERVATION_UNITS_UNAVAILABLE` with the units; the customer's quote acceptance and a paid checkout's confirmation answer as for stock that is gone, and name no unit. The customer's and the payment's never carry `overrideTurnoverBuffer` (their check has no place for it); the store's change carries it only for the buffer-only shortfalls of its lines, never for a held unit's conflict. A conflict that comes from the turnover buffer alone does not refuse the change: the change's activity row names those units (`turnoverBufferOnlyUnitIds`), which `reservations.get` returns with the activity; the dashboard's timeline does not show them, and the inbox does not list them (its `unit_conflict` compares a held unit with the unit's retirement and downtimes, never with another booking). The change's stock check still applies the buffer (S1). Database tests hold the three paths, both ways, and the store's path for a unit another booking's open choice needs and for a unit held past its line's quantity that another booking needs, with and without the flag (the first through an agent too); the three paths confirm an exact line holding a unit of another combination when nobody else needs it, and the flag books over a reservation's own unassigned units next to the units it holds. A unit given to a line with no exact combination is also checked against the stock: when another booking's open choice needs it, the assignment answers `errors.insufficientCapacity`. The stock check itself never follows a unit through the window: it counts per combination and places the others afresh at each instant (S1), and says only whether a held unit's combination has room for it next to the others, so this rule, by id, is what keeps one unit to one reservation. No test covers the unit conflicts of an assignment or of a date change.
Still open: an override covers the change that carries it, never a later one (D5 in [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md)). A later change that takes stock checks the buffer again; the store passes it with its own override, and the customer's acceptance and the paid confirmation, which cannot, are refused until the store confirms itself. What stays open is that the assignment and the move to another unit do not yet record their override on their own rows, nor the date edit the units it passed (it records the lines it passed since slice 5b there), and that a buffer-only conflict on a held unit accepted at a confirmation is not named to the store, in the history or in the inbox (section 5 there).

**S5. Consumable stock moves with the status.** A confirmation takes the quantity, a cancellation of a confirmed or ongoing reservation gives it back, a completion keeps it used. `changeReservationStatus` does it; an edit reconciles through `reconcileReservationStock`.
_Partial._ The status helper holds it, with database tests, and so does the dashboard's creation. Nothing tests that the dashboard's edit keeps calling the reconciliation.

**S6. A line blocks stock for the reservation's whole window.** The checkout accepts a period per line and prices each line on its own period, then stores only the reservation's dates: a helmet taken for one day of a four-day booking blocks four days.
_Open._ Either lines keep their own period, or the checkout stops accepting one. To decide with the convergence of the creation paths.

## Money

**M1. The totals are the sum of their parts.** The subtotal is the sum of the lines' totals, plus the insurance when it is not carried as a line. The total is what the tax engine gives for the lines, the delivery fee and the discount, tax included (`calculateTaxBreakdown`): with prices that include tax it is the subtotal plus delivery less the discount; with prices before tax, the tax comes on top. The deposit is never part of it.
**What the rental costs is its total.** A reservation's `total_amount` never contains its deposit, and what is owed for the rental is that total less the completed rental payments, net of their refunds (`getReservationRentalAmount`, `getRentalRemaining`, `apps/web/lib/reservations/util.rental-amount.ts`; the receivables figure reads the same in SQL). Zero is a valid total; a negative or unreadable one owes nothing. Migration 0108 made the older rows fit: it took the deposit out of exactly the totals that held it, each with a history line recording the total before. The billing snapshot keeps the reading it sends until the billing service decides on it.
_Held_ for the dashboard's edit and creation (slice 10 of [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md)): their totals are `computeReservationTotals` of their lines, insurance (exempt), delivery fee and discount, with the store's settings and the lines' products read under their locks, in the display mode the reservation recorded; each line's tax figures are the engine's. A line the edit does not change enters at its stored price (M3); a line it prices again from its product that no longer gets the same amount under the locks, or a renter or store position that moved since the insurer's quote and the routes, refuses it. `edit-totals.db.test.ts` compares every total and line tax figure with the engine's.
_Held_ for the date change of the API and agents: its totals are `computeReservationTotals` of the lines priced by `priceCart` under its locks, with the delivery fee and the deposit as stored, and its lines' tax figures the engine's; it writes only when the same engine gives the stored prices back over the stored window. `reservation-dates.db.test.ts` compares every written column with the engine's answer, with and before tax and with an exempt line.

**M2. A reservation has one currency, written on it when it is created.** `reservations.currency` holds it (`reservationCurrency`, `apps/web/lib/reservations/payment-billing-facts.ts`). Every amount stated, asked or held on that reservation is in it, whatever the store's settings say later. An amount sent against an existing provider object, the capture of a hold or the refund of a charge, is in that object's own currency, as the provider states it: a capture against a hold in another currency than the reservation's is refused, and the store captures it from Stripe.
_Partial._ What asks or holds money on an existing reservation reads the reservation's currency: the customer's payment session, the payment request, the dashboard's deposit hold, the customer's own deposit authorization and the extension quote. A capture is counted in its hold's currency, which Stripe is asked for, and a Stripe refund in its charge's; both are recorded as Stripe answered them. So does what states an amount to someone: the emails and text messages, the outbox payload of a status change (a row queued without the field states the store's, and so does a row queued with it but sent by an instance still running the previous code: the guarantee holds once every instance runs the new code), the contract, the invoice and credit note of a payment with no currency of its own, the marketplace's view of a booking, the inbox, and the dashboard's page and edit form of a reservation. A test fails when one of these reads the store's: the dashboard's hold and capture (`deposit-currency.db.test.ts`), the refund of an online payment (`stripe-refund.db.test.ts`), the payment session (`payment-session.db.test.ts`), the payment request (`util.payment-request.test.ts`, `actions.db.test.ts`), the extension quote (`extension-quote.test.ts`), each email that states an amount (`lib/email/send.test.ts`), the messages of a new request (`post-creation-effects.test.ts`), the status-change payload (`status-change-effects.test.ts`) and the marketplace view (`marketplace-booking.db.test.ts`). No test covers the customer's deposit authorization page, the contract, the invoice fallback, nor the dashboard's two pages. Still in the store's current currency, on screen and in read answers only: the dashboard's reservation list, calendar and planning, and its customer page; the list, calendar, planning, upcoming and overdue answers of the API (`services/reservations.ts`, one currency per answer, set in `routers/dashboard/reservation-follow-up.ts`; `reservations.get` gives the reservation's own); the customer account's pages, which format every amount with the store's currency (`useFormatMoney`). Rows created before the column existed hold null and fall back to the store's current currency, as does a stored value that is no currency code: a store that changes currency would restate all of them, until a backfill writes the currency they were made in.

**M3. A price is a fact of the booking.** The unit price, its breakdown, the tax rate, the promotion and the promo code applied when the line was priced are stored on the reservation. Only an edit of that line reprices it, and then with every rule the first pricing used. An edit of something else, or a change of the store's or the product's settings, changes no amount of an existing reservation.
_Partial._ The facts are recorded since migration 0107 (slice 9 of [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md)): the tax display mode on the reservation, the exemption and the promotion on each line, read through `packages/api/src/services/pricing-facts.ts`, where a fact never recorded reads as `"not_recorded"`. The checkout, the API's creation, its date change and the extension record or carry them; the dashboard's creation records the mode, the exemptions and, on a line priced from the catalogue, the promotion its price applied: it prices that line as the checkout does, with the product's promotion in force on the day of the booking; a line the dashboard's edit adds is priced and stored the same way, with the promotion in force at the edit. The dashboard's edit (slice 11) prices again only the lines it changes: a line it leaves as it was keeps its price, its breakdown and, when it recorded one, its tax rate (one that recorded none is taxed by today's settings, and the store is warned when that adds tax), and a line it prices again applies the promotion it recorded (`packages/api/src/services/edit-line-pricing.ts`, `edit-line-prices.db.test.ts`). Still open: a line priced again takes today's tax rate, not the one it recorded; the promo code is not recorded as a fact (P1).

**M4. Money has one representation.** An amount is a whole number of the currency's minor units, or a decimal string with exactly the currency's digits, and it is rounded once, where it is computed. A currency without decimals has none anywhere.
_Open._ Prices are JavaScript numbers rounded to two decimals in several places, and Stripe amounts are converted apart: a 50 % promotion on ¥101 is ¥50.50 in Louez and ¥51 at Stripe. Every writer stores a line priced from the catalogue alike, by one function (`catalogueLineValues`, `packages/api/src/services/price-cart.ts`): the checkout, the API's creation, the dashboard's creation and a line the dashboard's edit adds store that line as ¥50.50, its unit price and total to the cent, never rounded to the currency's minor unit; a price typed by hand in the dashboard's creation or edit is rounded once to the minor unit (¥100.5 typed is ¥101).
_Held_ for the tax engine (`calculateTaxBreakdown`, `computeReservationTotals`): it takes the reservation's currency and allocates and rounds the tax, the discount and the totals in its minor unit (¥101 at 10 % before tax is ¥10 of tax and ¥111 in all), for every writer that totals a reservation; two-decimal currencies compute exactly as before (`tax.test.ts`). A line's own price is still rounded as above.

**M5. A provider is asked once for one intent.** A charge, a hold, a capture or a refund is first recorded as an attempt under the reservation's lock, then sent with an idempotency key that is stable for that attempt. A retry after a lost answer finds the same provider object. A second caller finds the attempt and does not call. An attempt whose outcome is not known is never replaced by another.
_Partial._ The extension's checkout and refund follow it, and so does the store's deposit authorization (`apps/web/lib/reservations/deposit-hold.ts`): its attempt stays pending, is asked again with the same key, and is settled by a lookup on the connected account it was sent to or by the Connect webhook. It is closed only on what Stripe says: its PaymentIntent, a refusal of the request, or a complete listing of the PaymentIntents of the customer the attempt stored, made more than 24 hours after its last request, that does not hold it. A timeout, a 500 and a 500 replayed for the key close nothing, and neither does any age. Stripe promises no delay for making the PaymentIntent of a request it answered 500 to, so a closed attempt keeps its row (`failed`, with its reservation, account, amount, currency, card and id) and still reconciles: its PaymentIntent, when Stripe's event about it arrives and it matches the attempt, is recorded on that row, as the reservation's hold when it has none, or with its release owed when it has another. No background lookup reads a closed attempt again: that rests on the event's delivery. A wrong verdict of absence costs at most a second hold for a time, recorded once its event arrives, and released when another hold secures the reservation. An attempt nothing can settle (its customer cannot be listed, it has more PaymentIntents than a lookup reads, its request never reached Stripe) is shown by the dashboard and the inbox (`deposit_unconfirmed`), and the store closes it from the dashboard (`reservations.resolveDepositAttempt`, a session only): Louez looks the attempt up first, when it can, and records the PaymentIntent it finds; else the attempt is closed as above, on the store's word. An authorization the store sees in Stripe that Louez has not recorded for the attempt is the store's to cancel in Stripe (`deposit-hold.db.test.ts`, and a random walk over every event a hold can see, the store's closing included, `deposit-hold.model.db.test.ts`). Found is never absent: a PaymentIntent Louez finds for the attempt that the writer refuses (its amount, currency, card, customer, capture method or reservation are not the attempt's: it was altered at Stripe) closes and replaces nothing while Stripe may hold funds on it, whether the store's closing, a request or the closure a day after found it. The attempt stays pending, an activity line records who found it, when, which PaymentIntent and why it was refused, for that attempt's id, the inbox's `deposit_unconfirmed` names it, and the store is told to cancel it in Stripe (`errors.depositAttempt.needsStripe`; `DEPOSIT_ATTEMPT_NEEDS_STRIPE` from `reservations.resolveDepositAttempt`, with the PaymentIntent and the reason). That line blocks the attempt's closing as absent, under the reservation's lock: `closeAbsentAttempt` is the only closure of an attempt as absent, and the one this guard protects; an attempt also ends when the writer settles its own PaymentIntent (authorized, captured, cancelled, failed). A later lookup that fails, stops short or no longer lists the refused PaymentIntent proves nothing about it, and the closing as absent is made only when its caller brings that PaymentIntent read again by its id on the attempt's account, holding nothing (cancelled, or declined with nothing captured). A read that fails, or that shows it may hold funds, leaves the attempt pending and answers `needsStripe` again. The closing then records that it was read empty, and a new hold may be asked for; the line weighs on its own attempt only, and the row does not name that PaymentIntent, which the activity line keeps (`deposit-hold.db.test.ts`, for an altered amount and for removed reservation metadata, for a closing whose lookup fails or ends on an empty page that says more follow, a request Stripe refuses and the closure a day after, both with a complete listing that no longer shows it, a read by id that fails, and one that reads it cancelled). A captured one never holds nothing, so the request stays open. A request whose lookup tells nothing reads the attempt's row, under the reservation's lock, before it answers: one the webhook recorded meanwhile answers what was recorded. An attempt is claimed by one request at a time, and the claim has an owner: the request writes a random token of its own in `payments.claim_token`, with a lease in `claimed_until` on the database's clock, and another request takes the attempt over only once that lease has run out, under a token of its own. Claiming, renewing, giving back and every check that a request still holds its claim lock the reservation's row first, then the attempt's. The request renews its lease before each Stripe call, for one call at its longest; a renewal or a check that finds another token, or none (taken over, given back, or settled by the webhook, which clears it), has lost the claim. A request that lost it writes nothing of what it read from Stripe, calls Stripe no more, and answers from the attempt's row: in progress while the row names no PaymentIntent, else what was recorded there. Tests cover a renewal racing a takeover, a request whose lease ran out while another took the attempt over in the same second, and two claims of the same second (`deposit-hold.db.test.ts`). A deposit capture asks Stripe for its hold's currency first (M2), then sends a key that is stable for its PaymentIntent and amount and records no attempt: after a lost answer, the same capture asked again, or the webhook, records it. The release of a second authorization is an outbox row (`deposit.holdRelease`), added with the write that records it as owed. A refund of an online payment is an operation recorded before Stripe is asked (M9). The rental's sessions of an existing reservation, the customer's payment session and the store's payment request, go through one gate (`openRentalCheckoutSession`, `apps/web/lib/reservations/rental-session-gate.ts`): under the reservation's lock, the door's check (its statuses; for a rental request, its currency and that it asks no more than the rental still owed), then the rental's open payments cancelled; their sessions, and every earlier one Stripe has not said is over, expired or read at Stripe after the commit, a paid one recorded and nothing opened, an unknown one opening nothing; then the session created with an idempotency key (`checkout-session:<payment id>`, a fresh id for each call), which protects only the client's own retries of that one create, never two calls: two calls are kept apart by the lock and the install, not by the key; then, under the lock again, the same check, and the attempt installed as the `pending` `rental` row only when no other rental payment is open and no session may still be paid, else the session just created is expired. The row states what its session charges, in the currency's precision (a yen request of 100.60 charges and states 101), a rental request is compared with what is owed by that charge, and the completion writes on the row it claims the amount Stripe took. So a reservation has one open rental attempt, whichever door opened it, and sending a payment request again replaces its session. A replaced or expired session that took nothing leaves its row `cancelled`; once its session is settled (`session_settled_at`), the reservation's payment history in the dashboard does not list it (`paymentHistoryRows`, `util.payment-history.ts`), and the activity timeline keeps what happened. Only that display leaves it out: the totals, the exports and the API's payments (`reservations_get`, which states `sessionSettledAt`, and `list_payments`) list every row. A custom request takes the gate without the rental's balance: its payment is recorded as a `rental` row, which the rental's figures count. `payment-request-attempts.db.test.ts` (next to the payment request's page) and `payment-session.db.test.ts` cover both doors, each replacing the other, two at once, a payment recorded and a cancellation while the session is created. Not established: a create whose answer is lost, a create whose install transaction then fails, and a session the gate refused whose expire then fails each leave a session at Stripe that no row names; it can be paid until it expires. A session paid despite the gate is recorded once by its completion (`claimCompletedCheckoutPayment`, by its session id, on its row even when the gate cancelled it). A completion delivered again claims nothing: a row once paid, `completed` (netted by a partial refund) or `refunded` (given back in full), is claimed, and neither it nor its payment request is written again. What follows the claim depends on that row, read under the reservation's lock: while it is `completed`, the replay finishes what an earlier delivery left undone (the contract's validation, the marketplace attempt's confirmation, the invoice, the paid confirmation and its messages), the `payment_received` line and its messages staying the claiming delivery's alone; once it is `refunded`, the replay does none of it. The paid confirmation reads the payment again under its own locks, so a refund landing between the claim and the confirmation confirms nothing (`payment-completion.db.test.ts`, `complete-checkout-payment.db.test.ts`). The Connect webhook's fee record, made before the completion, is idempotent per reservation; and the reservation's page then reads the rental paid above its total, both payments listed; the refund goes through `refund_payment` (M9). No inbox item names an overpayment yet. The storefront checkout sends no key and records no attempt, and neither does the customer's own deposit authorization, which opens a new PaymentIntent each time its page asks for one and does not look at a pending store attempt: two holds can then exist, and the second one Louez learns of is recorded with its release owed, and released. A customer's authorization made on a connected account the store leaves before any confirmation or webhook recorded it has no row: the events of an account the store left are believed only for the rows that name it. Deleting an account deletes its stores' payment rows and outbox rows, and with them any release Louez still owed and the row a late event needs. Migration 0100 must be applied before this code serves requests: the app runs migrations at startup, and an instance of this code against the old schema fails its payment reads.

Louez itself creates exactly one PaymentIntent per attempt, under one idempotency key; what the deposit authorization leaves open each starts from a PaymentIntent altered or created by hand in the store's Stripe account:

1. one Louez had not found when its attempt was closed is refused when its event arrives, and only the logs say so;
2. one that read empty at the closure and was confirmed again afterwards is refused the same way;
3. one captured there keeps its attempt open, and Louez does not record that money (a manual deposit payment is capped at the deposit still to collect);
4. a second PaymentIntent given by hand the same reservation and attempt reference as an attempt, with matching amount, currency and card, settles that attempt when Louez finds it, whatever evidence exists about another unmatched PaymentIntent of that attempt, which then stays at Stripe untracked.

**M6. The balance is what is owed less what was received, in the reservation's currency.** A payment in another currency does not reduce it.
_Partial._ The statistics filter by currency (`services/sales-analytics.ts`). So do the payment request, the customer's payment session and the extension's paid check: they count a payment only when it is in the reservation's currency (`getSettledPaymentAmount` and `isRentalPaid` given that currency, `packages/utils/src/util.payment-movement.ts`). Only a Stripe payment states a currency of its own, since older rows recorded by hand kept the column's default, and a refund follows the payment it gives back. Tests cover the rule and its three callers (`util.payment-movement.test.ts`, `util.payment-request.test.ts`, `payment-session.db.test.ts`, `extension-quote.test.ts`). Still summing every payment whatever its currency: the dashboard's paid totals and payment summaries (`reservations-utils.ts`, `[id]/util.payment-refunds.ts`), the remaining amount a recorded payment is checked against (`recordPayment`), the customer account's paid amount and paid state, the API's reservation readers (`services/reservations.ts`) and the cancellation preview (`services/reservation-status.ts`). So the customer account can show as paid a reservation the payment session would still charge.

**M7. No more goes back than came in.** A refund never exceeds what its payment still holds, and a deposit return never exceeds the deposit received less what was already returned or captured. The check and the write are one step under the reservation's lock. A hold, its capture and its release are rows about the same provider intent.
_Partial._ A hold, its capture and its release have one writer (`settleDepositIntent`, `apps/web/lib/reservations/deposit-hold-writes.ts`), for the dashboard, the customer's page, the Connect webhook and the outbox alike. What it writes is a pure function of three facts: the state of the PaymentIntent's hold row, its state at Stripe, and what the reservation references (`util.deposit-hold-transition.ts`, tested pair by pair). A hold row follows Stripe and only moves forward; a PaymentIntent that is over always has its final row, so an older snapshot cannot reopen it; the reservation's deposit changes for the PaymentIntent it references, or takes an authorization or a capture when it holds none. The paid confirmation writes the session's customer, card and `depositStatus` only on a reservation on which no funds are held or were taken: when its `depositStatus` is `authorized` or `captured`, or one of its hold rows is `authorized` or `completed`, read after the reservation's lock, it leaves the three as stored (`paidConfirmationKeepsDeposit`, called from `packages/api/src/services/payment-reservation-status.ts`; `checkout-status.db.test.ts`). A `pending` reservation the paid confirmation left `card_saved` over an `authorized` hold row before that rule is left as it is, and on it the store can still take a second hold, which becomes the reservation's: the store's hold reads the saved card and `depositStatus`, not the ledger (open, [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md), section 5). Outside that state, a second authorization is never the reservation's hold: its row stays `authorized` with its release owed (`payments.release_owed_at`) until Stripe confirms the cancellation. The debt is what Louez owes, not its outbox row: the outbox asks Stripe until it passes, the cron gives the debt a new row a day after an abandoned or deleted one, and the inbox shows the debt after 30 minutes (`deposit_release_failed`). A failure Stripe reports for one PaymentIntent tells nobody while another holds the reservation's deposit. A PaymentIntent is written on a reservation only when a row names it, on the account that row names; or its attempt, open or closed, has no PaymentIntent yet and was sent to that account with its amount, currency and card; or it comes from the store's connected account of today: an account the store left writes nothing but on the rows that name it (`deposit-hold.db.test.ts`, `deposit-events.db.test.ts`). A row that names a PaymentIntent is believed for its account only: its amount, currency and card are not compared again, since rows written before this code may hold the column's default currency, and refusing the capture or the release of a recorded hold would leave money unrecorded. A refund of an online payment is bounded under the reservation's lock, in the transaction that records it (M9). `returnDeposit` and the recorded payments of the dashboard check their limit before their transaction. The agents' deposit return (`record_deposit_return` on MCP and the REST API) checks it under the reservation's lock, in the transaction it writes in (`packages/api/src/services/payment-records.ts`): two such returns sent together cannot exceed the returnable balance. That bounds the dedicated returns only; the balance is not conserved across every write. The agents' `record_payment` records a `deposit_return` with no limit at all, and deleting the deposit payment after a return (`delete_payment`, or the dashboard) leaves the balance negative. Both predate the move of these writes to procedures, which keeps them (open, for the money lot).

**M8. A refund given by hand is recorded against the one payment it gives back, and never moves money.** It is one positive `completed` row pointing at that payment (`refundOfPaymentId`), with its own method, in that payment's own currency (its stored currency, else its reservation's, else the store's: never the store's or the reservation's when the payment states another); the payment keeps its amount. Only a payment the store took by hand and can give back is refunded this way: one of the store's reservations, `completed`, a `rental`, `damage`, `adjustment` or `deposit_capture`, above zero, not itself a refund (a row with `refundOfPaymentId`, or a Stripe refund row, `isStripeRefundRow`), and not taken through Stripe, which has its own code. The amount is a plain decimal above zero with at most 8 digits before the point and 2 after (`paymentAmount`), with no non-zero digit past the minor unit of the payment's currency (none in yen, two in euros: `currencyMinorDigits`), and at most the payment's amount less every completed refund already linked to it, counted under the reservation's lock, then the payment's row: partial refunds follow one another up to that amount, and two refunds sent together never pass it between them. A payment in a three-decimal currency, which the billing contract does not carry, is refused (`REFUND_CURRENCY_NOT_SUPPORTED`). Every text that writes the amount (the preview, the refusals, the approval's page) writes it with exactly the digits it holds (`exactMoneyDecimals`). A recorded refund's credit note is owed by a durable record written with the refund, and produced once: an outbox row (`payment.refundCreditNote`) added in the refund's own transaction, which the invoicing issues once per refund however often it runs; the writer tries it once after the commit and answers `issued`, `pending` (owed, issued by the outbox) or `not_applicable` (the store issues no invoices, or the payment has none), never a success it did not have. An agent's refund needs a person's approval, bound to the payment, its reservation, the amount in the currency's minor units, the currency and the method, and to the payment's status and amount and the completed refunds linked to it: one recorded in between ends the approval (`state_changed`). Every refusal answers before anything is asked.
_Held._ One writer, `recordPaymentRefund` (`packages/api/src/services/payment-records.ts`), for the dashboard's refund dialog and `record_refund`; the app lends the row's insertion and the credit note (`ApiPorts.paymentRefunds`, `apps/web/lib/api/payment-refunds-port.ts`), and sends the outbox row (`apps/web/lib/invoicing/refund-credit-note-effects.ts`), which first checks that its payload names its own row's store and reservation and a refund of that payment, and otherwise gives the row up at once (`abandoned`, reported). `packages/mcp/src/payment-refunds.db.test.ts` covers each refusal, the precision of a yen amount and a three-decimal currency, the ceiling after an earlier refund, two refunds racing for the whole amount, a payment in another currency than its reservation's, the approval's binding, the outbox row and the answer's credit note, and the replay of an idempotency key; `apps/web/lib/invoicing/refund-credit-note-effects.db.test.ts` covers the credit note through the real outbox (a failure then a success, a crash after the commit, invoicing turned off, a payload that is not its row's); `confirmation-credentials.db.test.ts` covers who is asked. The rule bounds refunds given by hand; a Stripe refund is bounded by the charge, as M7 says.

**M9. A refund of an online payment is one durable operation.** It is recorded before Stripe is called, it reaches Stripe at most once whatever fails or is retried, and whichever of its sender and the webhook hears of it first, it is recorded once: one refund row, one credit note owed, one netting of the payment, one fee reversal. An operation is declared failed only on Stripe's own account of the charge, never on absence alone. What Louez records about a refund follows Stripe's final word. A payment taken through Stripe is refunded through Stripe; a refund recorded by hand (M8) is for payments taken by hand.
_Partial._ The operation and its service hold it (`apps/web/lib/reservations/stripe-refund.ts`, table `payment_refund_operations`, migration 0106). Agents reach it through `refund_payment` (`dashboard.payments.refund`), always with a person's approval for exactly that payment and amount, which the operation keeps (`approval_id`, unique); the dashboard has no screen for it yet and refuses a session ([API and agents](../architecture/api-and-agents.md)). A request runs in its caller's transaction, READ COMMITTED, under the reservation's row lock, then the payment's open operations, then the payment's row. It refuses, by a stable code: a payment of another store or none (`PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`); a refund row, a deposit hold (its capture is refundable, and goes back as a deposit return), money not taken online, a failed payment (`PAYMENT_NOT_REFUNDABLE`); a payment not settled or with no charge (`PAYMENT_NOT_SETTLED`); an amount that is not whole minor units above zero (`REFUND_AMOUNT_INVALID`); another currency than the charge's (`REFUND_CURRENCY_MISMATCH`); a three-decimal currency, which Louez's billing does not count (`REFUND_CURRENCY_NOT_SUPPORTED`, `currencyMinorDigits`); an open dispute (`PAYMENT_DISPUTED`); a charge recorded on payment rows of several reservations, whose locks no order of the other writers covers (`PAYMENT_CHARGE_SHARED`); no account on the payment's row nor on its store (`NO_STRIPE_ACCOUNT`); an approval already used (`REFUND_APPROVAL_USED`, also a unique index); and an amount past the ceiling (`REFUND_EXCEEDS_REFUNDABLE`, with what was paid, refunded, in flight and still refundable). The ceiling is the charge's, whichever of its payment rows is asked: every row of that account and charge is locked by id after the reservation, and the ceiling is the smaller of what Stripe says the charge still holds, read before the lock, and the payment's gross (the charge's capture when it has several rows) less the Stripe refunds recorded for any of them, once per refund, less the refunds recorded by hand against any of them before M8 refused them on a Stripe payment, and the open operations (`requested` and `unresolved`) on any of them. The manual writer (`recordPaymentRefund`) refuses a payment taken through Stripe (`REFUND_STRIPE_PAYMENT`), so an open operation's amount cannot be given back a second time by hand. It writes the operation `requested` and its outbox row (`payment.refund`) in that transaction. Stripe is reached after the commit, once inline with a short timeout, then by the outbox, through one sender, which claims the operation under a token of its own with a lease on the database's clock and writes the time of each request before it leaves. The request is `refunds.create` on the account and charge the payment names, with the key `refund:<operation id>` and the operation's id in the refund's metadata. The operation is the identity of a retry, never its amount: two refunds of one amount are two operations. A key whose first request is 23 hours old or more is never sent again: the operation is `unresolved`, and only Stripe's account of the charge settles it. That account is a fresh read of the charge and of all its refunds, page after page: it holds the refund of the operation (by its metadata), or holds none and the refunds Stripe counts (pending or succeeded) add up to what the charge says was refunded. Only then, and only after a request sent again was refused or more than 24 hours after the last request, is the operation `failed`. A listing that is incomplete, unreadable or does not add up closes nothing, and the amount stays held back. An unknown outcome (a timeout, a network error, a 5xx, a rate limit, a 500 replayed for the key) closes nothing: the sender throws, and no attempt, whoever makes it (the outbox, the cron's, a read of the refund), reaches Stripe before the time the last one set on the operation (`claimed_until`: a quarter of its age, at least 30 seconds, at most an hour, and while its key may still be sent never later than 23 hours after it was asked less one full attempt and the outbox's shortest retry, six minutes, so the wait never costs it its last request); the outbox asks again, and the cron gives an open operation whose outbox row was abandoned a new one (`requeueStaleRefundOperations`, bounded per run and in time). An operation still open a day after it was asked is shown in the inbox (`refund_unconfirmed`); no age closes it. The application fee is not refunded with the request: `charge.refunded` refunds the part of it the store no longer keeps, on what the charge captured, disputes counted (`fee-reversal.ts`). Louez states a refund as Stripe states it (`refundStateOf`): `succeeded`; `failed` for failed or canceled; `pending` for `pending`, `requires_action` or any status Louez does not know. The money follows Stripe's own count, which takes a refund into the charge's `amount_refunded` the moment Stripe makes it, pending or not, and gives it back if the refund fails: so does Louez. Once Stripe has made the refund, pending or not, the writer below writes its refund row, nets the payment and owes its credit note, in the same transaction and by the same idempotency as for a refund Stripe answered succeeded, whoever hears it first (the sender, `charge.refunded`, a refund event), and a refund made from Stripe's dashboard alike. While it is pending, every money figure reads it as given back: the payment's netted amount, `getRentalPaid`, the reservation's page, the billing snapshot and what is still refundable (the row counts it once; a pending operation is not an open one). Only the operation's stated status waits: it is `pending` with its refund and its row, and `refund_payment` and `get_refund` answer `pending` with `detail` `processing`, a refund issued that the bank is processing and the customer has not received yet. It becomes `succeeded` when Stripe says so, through `refund.updated` or `charge.refund.updated`, a read of the refund (`get_refund`, under the operation's wait) or the cron, which gives a `pending` operation a new outbox row an hour after its last, after the open ones, so its lookup runs even when no refund event is enabled on the Connect endpoint; a lookup that finds it still pending changes nothing, and a `pending` operation is never sent again. A refund that fails or is cancelled from pending goes through the failure path below, as one that failed after succeeding. Of two reports of one refund (the request's answer and the listing read after it, an event and the listing), the one further along stands (`laterRefundReport`: pending, then succeeded, then failed), whichever was read last. The application fee's share of a refund is refunded with `charge.refunded`, which Stripe sends when it makes the refund, pending or not; if that refund then fails, Stripe does not take the fee refund back, and the refund event's pass through the fee refund raises `fee_over_returned` (below). Not established: how long Stripe keeps a refund pending, and whether every pending refund ends with a refund event (the cron's lookup covers one that never comes); a refund the bank holds for days has no inbox item of its own: the reservation's page shows it among its payments as any refund, and `refund_unconfirmed` lists only `requested` and `unresolved` operations. Success is written by one writer (`recordChargeRefunds`, `stripe-refund-writes.ts`), for the sender and the webhook alike, in one transaction under the same locks: one refund row per refund (`ensureRefundPaymentRecord`, deduplicated by the refund's id), the credit note it is owed as an outbox row (`payment.refundCreditNote`, the one M8 uses), the payment netted from the capture and the refund rows that are `completed` once the listing is merged in, each refund once by its id (a refund of the oldest shape, with no row of its own, as Stripe lists it), so a listing older than what was recorded never moves it, and the operation the refund names settled from its refund's merged state. A refund made in Stripe's dashboard names none and is recorded the same way. A refund Stripe reports failed or canceled (`refund.failed`, `refund.updated`, `charge.refund.updated`, each handled only when enabled on the Connect endpoint, and `charge.refunded` when its listing shows it) is undone by the same writer, in one transaction: its row becomes `failed`, which every reader counts for nothing, the payment is netted again, its operation becomes `failed`, and an activity line names it. A refund that fails before Louez wrote a row of its own gets a row written `failed`, linked to the payment it came off (counts for nothing, owes no credit note), so the fact is kept under its id. "The row of a refund" is a row that is a refund (`isStripeRefundPaymentSql`), never the original payment the oldest webhooks stamped with its id: for such a refund the terminal row is added beside it, and the stamped payment and every legacy row keep their link, or their lack of one. A legacy refund with no row of its own counts as Stripe lists it only until a `failed` row exists for it. The writer reconciles a refund across every payment row of the reservation that holds the same charge, whichever of them the event names: the operation that made it, and the row it came off, which is netted again. The dashboard shows a `failed` refund row as failed, its amount struck through; the customer's pages and the contract leave it out. A failed refund is final in Louez: its row is never made `completed` again; a listing that later shows it moving money changes nothing and is named once for a person (`refund_undone`). The inbox shows the store every failed refund row (`refund_undone`, found from the refund row and the payment it gives back: by its link, or, for an older row with none, the payment of its reservation holding the same charge), whether or not Louez recorded it first: the customer was not refunded, so the store checks it in Stripe and refunds again if needed. A credit note already issued for it stands (invoicing has no document that cancels one): the inbox names it, to be corrected by hand. The credit-note generator tells a refund row from the payment it refunds by the same rule (`isStripeRefundRow`), and credits an older refund row with no link only against the one payment of its reservation that holds its charge on the same Stripe account (a row with no account is taken to be on the account of the charge's other rows, unless one of them names another): it issues a note for a refund of a payment the oldest webhooks stamped with another refund's id, and never one for that stamped payment. A refund's credit note stays owed until it is issued or does not apply. Its sender issues it for a `completed` refund row, ends with nothing to issue for a refund that failed or was cancelled, asks again later for one still pending, and gives up, reported, a payload that names no refund row. One the outbox gave up on while still owed (its refund row `completed`, no note yet) is shown in the inbox (`credit_note_not_issued`), kept past the outbox's retention, and armed again by the cron once a day (`rearmAbandonedEffects`) for 90 days after it was owed; past them it stays shown, for a person. Database tests hold each part of it, each proven to fail without the mechanism it tests (`stripe-refund.db.test.ts`, including a refund Stripe holds pending then succeeded (and what is left after it), one awaiting an action then failed, a pending refund made from Stripe's dashboard, an operation an earlier version left pending without its row, a request's answer and a listing that disagree, and one found pending by the lookup after a lost answer and settled by the cron's; `stripe-refunds-procedure.db.test.ts` for both tools' answers; `stripe-refund-inbox.db.test.ts`, `refund-credit-note-race.db.test.ts`, which races the real credit-note generator for one refund).
Still open: the extension's refund keeps its own key (`rental-extension-refund:<attempt>`) and is recorded by the webhook through the same writer, without an operation.
The application fee given back in excess on a charge (more than its share of the money the store no longer keeps, after a refund failed or a dispute was won) is Stripe's figure and the billing service's: Louez stores none. Stripe cannot take an application fee refund back. For a store the billing service bills, its ledger, which reads the fee and its refunds from Stripe and counts only the refunds that moved money, bills it again on the store's statement; for a store Louez's legacy billing bills, it stays given back. The fee refund at source compares, under the charge's lock, the fee given back with its share, and when it is above holds a candidate, written at once to the log as a warning marked unconfirmed. After the webhook answered, one task reads the fee, the charge's disputes, the charge and its disputes again, in that order, each with a 5-second timeout and no retry; if the disputes moved between its two reads it raises nothing (the dispute's own event reviews the charge), else it raises the billing alert `fee_over_returned` (`apps/web/lib/billing-sync/alerts.ts`) only if the excess on those fresh figures is still positive, naming the charge, the account, the fee and those amounts; a read that fails raises no alert and logs an error naming the charge. A charge whose fee is not above its share costs one comparison. A limit: a fee refunded by hand in Stripe's dashboard exactly between the confirmation's read of the fee and its read of the charge is not alerted, and Louez has no handler for `application_fee.refunded` to look at it again; the billing service hears that event and records the fee refund.

## Status

**T1. One function changes a status, one inserts.** `changeReservationStatus` locks the row, checks the transition, moves consumable stock, dates `billableAt` and `withdrawnAt`, and writes the activity row, under a lock token of the owner entry (C1). `insertReservation` is the application's only insert; the token it returns allows one use, the new reservation's stock taken once. `scripts/reservation-writes.test.mjs` fails on a write in another form ([Data layer, DL-12](../code-review/04-data-layer.md)); it reads syntax, exempts the seeds, and does not see the store-cloning script, the billing-date backfill or dynamic SQL.
_Held_ for application code.

**T2. The transitions are a fixed graph.** `pending` goes to `confirmed`, `rejected` or `cancelled`; `quote` to `confirmed`, `declined` or `cancelled`; `confirmed` to `ongoing` or `cancelled`; `ongoing` to `completed` or `cancelled`. Nothing leaves `completed`, `cancelled`, `rejected` or `declined`, and nothing enters `pending` or `quote` (`packages/db/src/consumable-stock.ts`).
_Held._

**T3. A completed reservation is not edited.** Its lines, dates and totals are what was rented. Neither is a cancelled, rejected or declined one.
_Held_ for every edit, with one rule (`isReservationEditable`, `packages/api/src/services/reservation-editable.ts`): only `pending`, `quote`, `confirmed` and `ongoing` are changed, read before the lock and again under it. The dashboard's edit (`updateReservation`) answers `errors.reservationStatusChanged` and its edit page does not open another status; the date change of the API and agents (`changeReservationDates`) answers `RESERVATION_NOT_EDITABLE`. `update-reservation.db.test.ts` and `reservation-dates.db.test.ts` cover each other status and a completion or a cancellation that lands while the change waits.

**T4. A reservation's number is unique in its store.**
_Open._ The dashboard numbers with a count of the year's reservations plus one, read with no lock; the online store draws a number and checks it before the insert, a race its own comment admits (`packages/api/src/services/reservation-number.ts`). The table has no unique index on store and number.

**T5. A reservation's window is a real interval of the store's time.** The end is after the start, and a local date and time the customer or the store typed is one that exists in the store's time zone.
_Partial._ The extension checks the time zone round trip, nonexistent local times included. `updateReservation` refuses an end that is not after the start (`update-reservation.db.test.ts`). The forms built on the date picker, the dashboard's edit included, do not check the round trip yet, and move a time the clocks skip forward.
_Held_ for the date change of the API and agents: it takes instants with their offset (no local time to round-trip), refuses an end that is not after the start, and an ongoing reservation's start that moves (`RESERVATION_WINDOW_INVALID`); `reservation-dates.db.test.ts` covers both.

**T6. Pickup follows the contract's rules.** Validating the contract and signing it are two steps (`services/reservation-contract-validation.ts`); a store that requires a signature refuses a pickup without one (`services/reservation-status.ts`).
_Held_ for the store's status changes.

## Promo codes

**P1. A promo code is used as often as its limit says.** Its validity, its limit and what it may combine with are checked when it is applied, and its use is counted in the booking's transaction.
_Partial._ The evaluation (`packages/api/src/services/promo.ts`) and the counting (`apps/web/lib/reservations/apply-promo-code.ts`) are separate steps. To review with the creation lot.

## Extensions

**X1. An extension holds stock while its checkout is live, and is applied only to the reservation it was quoted for.** A payment that completes against a reservation that changed since the quote, or whose stock is gone, is refunded and not applied (`extension-service.ts`).
_Partial._ The extension service does it. Its tests of the payment's completion and refund replace the transaction with a mock (`extension-service.test.ts`). On a database, `create-manual-reservation.db.test.ts` checks that a live extension checkout holds the stock to the end it asks for and an expired one holds nothing, `required-accessories.db.test.ts` runs the extension's stock check, and `writers-lock-order.db.test.ts` starts an extension against the store's cancellation. No database test completes a payment against a reservation that changed since its quote.

## Concurrency

**C1. Locks are taken in one order.** The store row, then products by id, then reservations, then units by id ([Data layer, Lock order](../code-review/04-data-layer.md)). Every status change takes its locks through the owner entry of `@louez/db` (`packages/db/src/reservation-change.ts`), READ COMMITTED, and runs the whole transaction again on a lost deadlock or a product added meanwhile, three runs in all. A change that moves stock or runs a stock check locks the reservation's products, then the reservation (`runReservationChange`); the customer's decline and cancellation, the marketplace's expiry and the failed or abandoned checkout, which move none, lock the reservation alone (`runReservationOnlyChange`); the paid confirmation and the marketplace's cancellation choose from the status they read (`runReservationChangeByStatus`), and start over when they took the reservation alone and find another status under it. The store's own change locks the products for every change, whether it moves stock or not. The unit assignment, the product page's move to another unit and the extension's quote run in the same owner entry and take the same lock (`lockReservationInTransaction`) before they lock any unit. The account deletion locks its stores' products by id, then their reservations, then deletes their rows.
_Held_ for status changes: `changeReservationStatus` and the consumable stock helpers run only under a lock token the owner entry issues for the transaction, the store and the reservation, so a status change cannot be written without its locks. What a token allows is what the module recorded when it issued it, never what its fields say, and an owner entry's token stops working when its transaction ends. An `insertReservation` token issued in a transaction the owner entry did not open (the dashboard's creation) is valid there by convention only, and allows one stock taking. A call without the token does not compile, throws at run time, and fails `scripts/reservation-writes.test.mjs` (R6); `packages/db/src/reservation-change.db.test.ts` fails when the entry stops locking the products before the reservation, or accepts another transaction's token, a rewritten or copied token, or an inserted token twice; `packages/api/src/services/marketplace-booking.db.test.ts` fails when cancelling a `pending` marketplace booking takes its products' lock; `apps/web/lib/reservations/status-change-races.db.test.ts` fails when a status writer racing the store's change starts a run over; `apps/web/lib/reservations/writers-lock-order.db.test.ts` fails when the unit assignment, the move to another unit or the extension, racing the store's change in either order, deadlocks or starts a run over; `apps/web/lib/account-deletion/database-repository.db.test.ts` fails when the account deletion takes a reservation before its product or makes a run start over, racing any of those three writers in either order, or a status change that moves stock and holds its product when the deletion starts; `packages/db/src/product-lock.db.test.ts` fails when the shared product locks (`lockProductRows`, `lockStoreProductRows`) meet a catalog's rows in another order than by id, on a catalog whose slugs run opposite to its ids; `packages/api/src/routers/dashboard/products.db.test.ts` fails when a reorder of the catalog writes a product before it holds the ones of lower id it moves, or when a product's save or creation holds a product before the lowest accessory it names, and `categories.db.test.ts` beside it when a category's deletion writes a link before it holds its products. No test fails when a new path locks several products without those two functions. _Partial_ for the rest: the dashboard's edit follows it through its own code, and no test fails when a new path inverts it.

**C2. A write decides on what it reads under its locks.** Whatever it read before them it reads again, or it proves nothing changed, before its first write.
_Partial._ The checkout rechecks stock and required accessories under the product locks, and the store's status changes read everything under theirs. The manual creation decides its stock on what it reads under the product locks: the products, the store's availability rules and the combination each line chose. The checkout still takes those rules from a read made before its locks, and both still write the prices they read before locking. `updateReservation` computes before its locks too, then proves under them that nothing it read has changed (`fingerprintReservationEdit`), or refuses the edit. The edit form's own read is proved the same way: the form sends back the token of the reservation it loaded, and the edit refuses when the reservation under its locks is no longer that one (slice 11 of [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md)). The dashboard's recorded payments and deposit returns check their limits before their transaction (M7).

A reservation is never created for a customer who no longer exists: the checkout reads its customer under the row's lock, after its products, in the transaction that inserts the reservation (`upsertCustomer`), and a customer's deletion takes the store row, then the customer, and counts their reservations under that lock before it deletes anything (`deleteCustomer`); `apps/web/lib/reservations/checkout-customer-lock.db.test.ts` holds both orders. The dashboard's manual creation still reads its customer before its transaction.

**C3. No effect runs inside a transaction that may run again.** A fee, an email, a notification or a provider call runs after the commit, once.
_Held_ for the paths listed under the lock order: the work each status writer gives the owner entry writes rows only (the store's messages go to the outbox in it), and its fees, messages, Stripe calls, insurance and revalidation run after it returns. Established by reading: no test can see an effect inside a transaction.

## Effects

**E1. A message that must not be lost is a row of the outbox, added in the write's transaction** ([The outbox](../architecture/api-and-agents.md#the-outbox)).
_Partial._ The store's status changes do, and so does the date change of the API and agents (`reservation.modifiedEmail`). The messages of a new booking, and those of a customer's quote acceptance or cancellation, are sent after the commit from memory: a crash between the two loses them. The extension's messages have their own recoverable attempts, outside the outbox.

**E2. A write sent twice with the same idempotency key runs once.**
_Partial._ A procedure built on `storeProcedure` replays a recorded success and refuses a concurrent or unknown outcome (`runOperation`). A call that ends on a refusal (`isRefusal`) is `failed` and its key runs it again; a call that ends on any other error is `unknown` and its key never runs it again, so a handler that threw after its commit is not run twice. The rule rests on a convention: a handler raises a refusal only when nothing it wrote remains ([Raising a refusal](../architecture/api-and-agents.md#writes-audit-idempotency-receipts)). A record marked `failed` before that rule, also when its handler threw after its commit, keeps its status, and its key runs it again. A procedure with a secret output ignores the key. The server actions that are not procedures yet have none.

## Test reservations

A store owner rehearses the customer's journey on their own store with a test reservation (`reservations.is_test`, its customer `customers.is_test`, migration 0110; [glossary](glossary.md)). A store has at most one (`reservations_one_test_per_store`). The rules below hold for it whoever writes; each place that applies one names test reservations in a one-line comment and uses the helpers of `packages/db/src/test-reservations.ts` (`excludeTestReservations`, `excludeTestCustomers`, `isTestReservation`, `refuseOnTestReservation`) or reads `isTest`, so the feature is found, and removed, by searching for `TestReservation` and `isTest`.

**TR1. A test reservation holds no stock.** It is left out of the availability rule (`buildReservationAvailabilityPredicate`, so `findBusyUnitIds`, `reserveInventory`, the storefront's and the marketplace's availability, and the consumables' reserved quantities), and every reader that counts what blocks stock by status leaves it out too: unit conflicts and their inbox items, a unit's deletion and the unit count a product edit keeps, turning unit tracking off, the stock kind's change, the dashboard's availability warnings, the product page's utilization, `check_availability` on MCP and in the assistant. It never takes or gives back a consumable (`mutateReservationStock`): an exception to S5. The test reservation itself is checked against the real stock when it is created or confirmed, as any booking; only the other rows are read.
_Partial._ Held by reading; `packages/api/src/services/test-reservations.db.test.ts` checks the busy units and the unit conflicts against a real booking.

**TR2. A test reservation is never billed.** No `billable_at`, no fee, no card-gate check, no billing mark and no snapshot (`insertReservation`, `changeReservationStatus`, `markReservationIds`, `buildReservationSnapshot`); the billing heartbeat does not count it among the billable rows that lack a `billable_at`. Its payment is simulated: no Stripe call, and it counts in no revenue.
_Partial._ Established by reading. `reservations:backfill-billable-at --apply` reruns the SQL of migration 0083 and would date a confirmed test reservation: run it only once none is left.

**TR3. A test reservation counts nowhere.** The home's counters and today's departures, returns and requests, the sales and traffic figures, the multi-store figures, the plan's usage, the launch milestones (`firstReservation`, `firstCompletedReservation`), the inbox, the exports, the ICS feed and Google Calendar, the reminders, the admin digest, the review requests and the abandoned-checkout pass leave it out, and the test customer out of every customer count. The customers list counts a test reservation only on its test customer.
_Partial._ The database test checks the home's counters, the reservations list's counts and the launch milestones.

**TR4. Only a person in the dashboard sees it.** A session that is not the in-app assistant (`canSeeTestReservations`, `packages/api/src/platform/test-reservation-visibility.ts`) sees the test reservation and its test customer, with a badge. A key, an OAuth or MCP connection and the assistant read as if they did not exist: the lists, the counters, the poll, the calendar, the planning, the agenda, the search, the product's timeline and a unit's history leave them out (`ReservationReader.testReservations`), and a call that names one by id (`reservations_get`, a status change, a date change, the notes, a payment, a deposit return, a refund) answers the not found it answers for an id the store does not have, before anything is asked or written. The hand-written MCP tools, resources and prompts and the assistant's own tools always leave them out.
_Partial._ The database test reads the list and the reservation through an API key and through a session.

**TR5. Only the owner who made it gets its messages.** Its customer is that owner (the checkout writes the owner's account e-mail on it); its e-mails are tagged "Test" in subject and body, and it sends no text message, push or platform notice (`apps/web/lib/test-reservations/message-policy.ts`, read from `is_test` when a message goes out).
_Partial._ Established by reading.

## The API's creation

What `createStoreReservation` holds, each with its database test in `packages/api/src/routers/dashboard/reservation-create.db.test.ts` unless named (slice 14 of [Reservation changes](reservation-changes.md)):

- **S1, S2.** It allocates with `reserveInventory` under the product locks, without the buffer then with it, as a confirmation does, and refuses a cart without its required accessories.
- **S3, S4.** `allowOverbooking` passes only what the stock itself lacks, `overrideTurnoverBuffer` only what the buffer alone stops; the verdict counts `overbooked` without the buffer; a consumable short on hand refuses whatever the flags; a key's or a token's flag needs a person's approval, bound to the shortfalls.
- **S5.** A booking created confirmed takes its consumables (`consumeReservationStock`).
- **M1, M2, M3.** Its totals are the tax engine's for its lines (`computeReservationTotals`), its currency is written on it at creation, and its lines keep the checkout's pricing facts: `reservation-create-parity.db.test.ts` compares them with the checkout's, cart against cart.
- **T1.** It inserts through `insertReservation`, and `scripts/reservation-writes.test.mjs` reads it as any writer.
- **T4.** Its number is drawn and checked in its transaction, under the store row's lock: two of its creations never share one. Against the checkout and the dashboard's creation, which take no such lock, T4 stays open.
- **T5.** Its window must end after it starts; ISO instants with an offset always exist.
- **C1, C2.** The store row, then the products by id (`lockReservationProducts`), then the customer, then its own rows and the outbox; it prices and checks on what it reads under them. A customer's deletion takes the store row then the customer, and counts their reservations under that lock.
- **C3, E1.** Its messages are outbox rows of its transaction; nothing else leaves before the commit.
- **E2.** It requires an idempotency key from keys and tokens.

## Measuring

Each rule about data at rest can be counted with a read-only query: totals against lines, reservations over the stock, holds per reservation, currencies, numbers used twice. Run the count on real data before and after a rule moves to held, to see that the writers stopped producing violations. Keep the figures out of this repository.
