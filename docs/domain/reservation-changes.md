# Reservation changes

What changes a reservation once it exists: its status, changed by any actor, and its shape (dates, lines, prices, delivery), changed by the store's edit. This page specifies the lot that moves these writes to one service per kind of write in `packages/api`, on RPC, OpenAPI and MCP. It covers what the code does today, what has been decided, what stays open, the fixed rules, and the slices in their order. The rules every writer holds are in [Reservation invariants](reservation-invariants.md), and the words are in the [glossary](glossary.md).

Every sentence about the code is true at `origin/rental-core` 3b011bfc and cites where, except where slice 1 (section 7) changed the status writers' locks, slice 2 what a confirmation records, slice 3 where required accessories are checked, slice 4 the store's confirmation over its stock and slice 5 its confirmation past the turnover buffer: those sentences are true after them. A sentence that proposes is marked **Proposed**. Line numbers drift: when one no longer matches, search the quoted name.

Two standing rules frame every proposal:

1. **No dead end for the store on short capacity.** When a store-side write is refused because quantities are short (returnable stock, or the turnover buffer), the store can override it explicitly, and the override is recorded with the write. The two overrides are separate: `allowOverbooking` covers a capacity shortfall, and `overrideTurnoverBuffer` a buffer-only one. Each covers the write that carries it, never a later one (D5). A physical conflict is never overridable: a unit held by two blocking reservations at once (S4), consumable stock below zero (S3), or a unit another booking's open choice needs (section 6). The store is never stuck, because it can record the booking without that unit, or with another one. A customer, a payment, the marketplace and the system never get an override.
2. **Fixed named rules, not settings.** Each business rule is one named function whose behaviour is what the code does today, unless this page decides otherwise. A setting exists only where one exists already (section 6).

## 1. The writers, as the code is today

`changeReservationStatus` (`packages/db/src/reservation-status.ts:277`) is under every status change. It runs only under a lock token of the owner entry (slice 1), and it locks the reservation row (`:286`), plans the change from the locked status (`planReservationStatusChange`, `:190`), and asks the card gate for a change into the billable set (`confirmed`, `ongoing`, `completed`) from outside it, unless the actor is `payment` (`requiresReservationGate`, `:155`; called at `:286`). It then runs the caller's `beforeWrite` (`:291`) and moves consumable stock (`:293`: `consume` from `pending` or `quote` to `confirmed`, `restore` from `confirmed` or `ongoing` to `cancelled` or `rejected`, `:180`). It writes the status and its dates, writes one activity row (`:314`) and marks the billing snapshot (`:320`). It returns the fee effects, and the caller runs them after the commit (section 4). Consumable stock locks the reservation's consumable products after the reservation (`packages/db/src/consumable-stock.ts:220`).

The stock check of a change into `confirmed` is `checkConfirmationStock` (`packages/api/src/services/reservation-inventory.ts:756`), behind two entries since slice 4: `checkExistingReservationInventory` (`:905`) for the customer's acceptance and the paid confirmation, `checkStoreConfirmationInventory` (`:930`) for the store's change. It reads the reservation's lines in a fixed order (`:764`), locks their products, and runs `reserveInventory` without the consumable check, without the required-accessory check since slice 3, and with `onShortfall: "collect"` since slice 2: once with the turnover buffer (`:814`) and, when the store keeps one, once without it (`:817`). It then checks each held unit by id (`findReservationUnitConflicts`). A unit's hard conflict throws `ReservationUnitConflictError` (`:825`), before any stock refusal and for every caller; so does, next, a held unit the other bookings need (`:830`, slice 4). A line short without the buffer is a capacity shortfall; a line short only with it, a buffer-only shortfall. The customer's and the payment's entry refuses either with `ReservationInventoryError("errors.productNoLongerAvailable")`, as before: a shortfall counted with the buffer, which names no line. The store's entry refuses a buffer-only shortfall with `ReservationTurnoverBufferShortfallError` unless it carries `overrideTurnoverBuffer` (slice 5), then a capacity shortfall with `ReservationCapacityShortfallError` unless it carries `allowOverbooking`; neither flag passes the other's shortfalls. The unit check does not reject a buffer-only conflict: it returns those units as `turnoverBufferOnlyUnitIds` for the activity row. Both entries return the capacity verdict of the demand checked (slice 2): the capacity shortfalls the store booked over, or none, and the date of the row that carries it. The store's entry, sent with `overrideTurnoverBuffer`, also returns the buffer-only shortfalls it passed and the buffer it checked them with (slice 5).

The paid checkout (`runCheckoutPaymentCompletion`, `complete-checkout-payment.ts:300`) commits in separate steps, in this order:

1. **Payment claim**, its own commit (`claimCompletedCheckoutPayment`, `:338`): once per reservation and session, whichever of the webhook and the customer's return comes first.
2. **Contract validation** `payment`, its own transaction (`:362`).
3. **Marketplace attempt confirmed**, for a marketplace booking (`confirmMarketplaceBookingAttempt`, `:366`). This happens before the status transaction, so an attempt can be `confirmed` while its reservation stays `pending` after a stock refusal.
4. **Invoice** generated for the payment (`:377`). For a claimed payment: a `payment_received` activity row, and the store's payment notifications sent from memory (`:379`–`:411`).
5. **Payment details** written on the pending row outside any transaction (`:152`), then the **status transaction** (`:177`) with its stock check. A refusal is logged, and the reservation stays `pending` (`:238`, `:242`). The refusal is `ReservationInventoryError` (a unit conflict included, `reservation-inventory.ts:575`) or `ConsumableStockError`.
6. **After a confirmation**, from memory: the store's confirmation notifications (`:486`), the invoice attachments and contract signature link prepared (`:496`), the customer's `customer_reservation_confirmed` (`:497`), and calendar sync queued (`:544`).

No hand-written MCP tool writes a status: `packages/mcp/src/tools/` holds calendar, customers and payments only. The marketplace's holds create a booking through the checkout's `createReservation` (`marketplace-booking.ts:700`) and change no status.

Where the status writers differ:

- **Lock order.** Since slice 1 every status writer locks through the owner entry: products, then the reservation, or the reservation alone for those that move no stock. They no longer differ.
- **Override.** Only the store's change can book over returnable stock at a confirmation (slice 4), as the store's dashboard creation and edit can (S3), the edit only on products whose demand it raises; and only the store's change can confirm past the turnover buffer (slice 5), as its assignment, move and date edit can pass it (S4). The customer's acceptance and the paid confirmation call an entry that takes no override (`scripts/reservation-writes.test.mjs`, R7, keeps the store's entry to the store's service).
- **What a confirmation records.** Since slice 2, every confirmation that checks the stock writes the verdict of its check on its activity row, as the dashboard's creation and edit do (`apps/web/app/(dashboard)/dashboard/reservations/actions.ts:1077`, `:2886`), next to `turnoverBufferOnlyUnitIds`. They no longer differ.
- **Required accessories.** The dashboard's creation and edit skip them (`actions.ts:942`, `:2872`), and since slice 3 no confirmation checks them (`reservation-inventory.ts:739`): they are checked where a cart is composed, by the checkout and the extension. They no longer differ.
- **Effects.** The store's changes queue outbox rows in their transaction. The customer's, the payment's and the marketplace's send from memory after the commit (E1).
- **Insurance.** The store's confirmation attempts the Tulip contract through `ports.insurance.createContract` after the commit (`reservation-status.ts:617`). The attempt may be skipped or fail, a past start included: `tulipContractStatus` is set to `failed` and the error `errors.tulipContractPastDate` is raised (`apps/web/lib/integrations/tulip/contracts.ts:725`–`:732`). The warning comes back in the store's answer. The customer's acceptance and the paid confirmation attempt none (by reading).
- **Date-change requests.** The store's changes settle them; the marketplace's cancellation does not.

Which statuses can be edited depends on the path:

- `updateReservation` refuses only a `completed` reservation (`actions.ts:1806`).
- The edit context the page opens refuses `completed`, `cancelled` and `rejected`, but allows `declined` (`packages/api/src/services/reservation-edit-context.ts:16`).
- A call that bypasses the page can also edit a cancelled or rejected reservation, since the fingerprint only requires the status to be unchanged.

When submitted lines are not empty, they replace the lines left out. An empty list takes the unchanged-lines branch (`actions.ts:1898`).

The edit's total is `subtotal + delivery − discount` (`:2525`), without the tax engine. It reprices a line through `calculateSeasonalAwarePrice` alone (`:1980`, `:2128`), without the product's promotion that the checkout applies (`apps/web/lib/reservations/price-cart.ts:126`, `packages/api/src/services/pricing-catalog.ts:286`).

The unit assignment (`assignUnitsToReservationItem`, `actions.ts:4637`) locks the reservation, then the product, then the units, and retries once (`:5038`). It checks stock twice:

- Without the buffer first. A shortfall answers `errors.insufficientCapacity`, with no override (`:4879`): another booking's open choice needs the unit.
- Then with the buffer. A shortfall answers `errors.turnoverBufferConflict` unless `overrideTurnoverBuffer` (`:4884`, `:4967`).

The override is written nowhere: `reservation_item_units` has no column for it (`packages/db/src/schema.ts:3290`), and the unit event's payload holds only the reservation and the line (`actions.ts:4684`). The assignment deletes every assignment of the line and inserts the selected units with new ids, including the units it keeps (`:5019`, `:5023`). Deleting a unit sets its assignments' `product_unit_id` to null (`schema.ts:3321`). The product page's move to another unit (`apps/web/app/(dashboard)/dashboard/products/[id]/actions.ts:345`, `:421`) behaves the same.

**A writer outside this lot.** Product and variant configuration can rewrite the combination keys of booked lines. `syncProductCombinationKeys` (`packages/api/src/services/variant-axes.ts:152`) rekeys `reservation_items.combination_key` when a product's booking axes change (`services/products.ts:1317`), and so does withdrawing a variant from the store (`removeVariantFromStoreProducts`, `variant-axes.ts:209`, `:265`). That writer stays out of this lot. Any confirmation bound to a line's choice must treat a rekeyed line as changed (slice 12).

## 2. The state table

The graph is `packages/db/src/consumable-stock.ts:36` (T2). The columns are:

- **Who** may make the change.
- **Preconditions**: what must hold.
- **Checked under**: which locks the check runs under.
- **Written**: what is written besides the status, dates, activity and billing mark, which the helper always writes.

## 3. Decisions

Taken by the program's operator after review, for this lot.

**D1. A customer cannot accept a quote the store created over the stock while the shortfall lasts.** The acceptance is refused as today. The store confirms the quote itself, with the override of slice 4.

- Reason: carrying the store's approval into the customer's acceptance needs approval evidence bound to the quote's revision and its canonical demand. It also needs an exact "no worse" comparison per demand identity, over time segments. Neither exists:
  - shortfalls are per line;
  - a pooled line's shortfall has `combinationKey: null` (`reservation-inventory.ts:482`);
  - a quote edit writes no fresh verdict, because a quote does not hold stock, so it skips the edit's stock check (`actions.ts:2856`).
- Strongest objection: the store already decided, and the customer's acceptance fails on a decision that is not theirs. The answer is that a comparison of aggregate shortfalls would let a new problem through. An approved shortage of 1 on size M, with M freed and L short by 1, gives an equal aggregate and an unapproved shortage on L.
- Possible later slice, not in this lot: approval evidence bound to the quote's revision and to stable demand identities, compared per time segment.

**D2. API keys and agents may book over the stock.** The owner delegates that authority when granting write access. A call with `allowOverbooking` always asks for confirmation (`requireConfirmation({ always: true })`). Its preview lists every shortfall, and its token is bound to the current verdict (slice 4). The credential is recorded on the activity row.

- What this proves: the caller saw the preview, not that a human approved it. The same agent can send both calls (`packages/api/src/platform/confirmation.ts:40`).
- Strongest objection: an unattended agent could approve shortages again and again while it optimises bookings. A store that wants a human in the loop withholds write access until owner hand-offs exist.

**D3. A buffer-only conflict on a held unit does not refuse a customer's or a payment's confirmation.** This is today's behaviour, kept: the unit check accepts a unit that only the turnover buffer around another booking reaches, and the activity row keeps its id (`turnoverBufferOnlyUnitIds`). The buffered capacity check still runs and can still refuse such a confirmation (section 1). Neither writer is promised acceptance. Naming the conflict to the store in the activity row, and an inbox item, are open (section 5).

- Strongest objection: the buffer may be needed for cleaning or charging, so accepting it may promise what the store cannot deliver. Until the open item is settled, the store does not learn of it before pickup.

**D4. The quote-acceptance message links to the reservation's page in the customer account, which shows the current balance and opens the payment.** No message sender opens a Stripe session.

- Reason: `createReservationPaymentSessionForCustomer` opens a new session on every call (`apps/web/lib/reservations/payment-session.ts:33`), so a sender retrying from the outbox would leave orphan sessions.
- Strongest objection: a delayed message read on another device adds a sign-in, and the page shows the current amount, which may differ from the quote the customer accepted.
- Ordering: while the acceptance itself still opens a session, the session-before-fee order stays (`.../actions.ts:262`, `:278`). A page link does not make that session idempotent.

**D5. A turnover-buffer override gives no later authority.** It is the store's act on the change that carries it, recorded on that change. A later change that takes stock checks the buffer again: the store passes it with its own override; a customer's acceptance and a payment's confirmation cannot, as for a booking over the stock (D1), and the store then confirms itself.

- Reason: trusting an earlier override at a later confirmation needs approval evidence bound to the demand as the allocator reads it, and to what the store was shown. The demand is the current axes, each unit's combination, the line's set of units and its unassigned remainder. None of it exists. A first design stored approvals per unit and per line: it could put one back in force for a demand the store never saw, since an axis removed leaves the stored choice unchanged and the demand wider.
- Strongest objection: a store that assigned a unit inside a buffer on purpose, then receives the customer's payment, has to confirm by hand a booking it already decided on.
- Possible later slices, not in this lot: stored approvals with a frozen effective demand; or a store setting that makes the buffer advisory for automatic confirmations.

## 5. Open, not in this lot

- **Consent to the quote's latest revision.** The customer accepts whatever the quote holds under the lock (`acceptQuote`, `.../actions.ts:200`). Nothing proves the customer saw that revision's price.
- **Signed contracts and issued invoices after an edit.** Contract validation returns at once when the reservation is already validated, and never invalidates a signature (`packages/api/src/services/reservation-contract-validation.ts:23`). An edit that changes dates, lines or amounts leaves the signed contract and the issued invoice as they were. Amendment, re-signature and document versions are not decided.
- **An expired checkout whose cancellation fails is never cancelled.** The Connect webhook's `checkout.session.expired` commits the session's pending payment as cancelled first, then cancels the abandoned checkout (`apps/web/app/api/webhooks/stripe/connect/route.ts:585`–`:625`). When that cancellation throws (a busy reservation, as a lost deadlock before slice 1), the webhook answers 500 and Stripe sends the event again; the redelivery reads the cancelled payment, concludes the checkout was not abandoned, and skips the cancellation. The reservation stays `pending`, holding its stock while `pendingBlocksAvailability` is on, until the store cancels it. It is so on `rental-core` before slice 1 too.
- **A buffer-only conflict on a held unit, accepted at a confirmation.** The customer's acceptance, the paid confirmation and the store's change accept a unit that only the turnover buffer around another booking reaches (D3, S4). Their activity row keeps the unit's id (`turnoverBufferOnlyUnitIds`), which `reservations.get` returns, but nothing names it to the store as a buffer conflict: the dashboard's history does not show it, and no inbox item lists it before pickup (the inbox's `unit_conflict` compares a held unit with its retirement and downtimes, never with another booking). Which inbox source, what resolves it, and how the history shows it are not decided.
- **A shortfall only the turnover buffer makes, recorded as an overbooking by the edit and the creation.** Both check the stock once, with the store's turnover buffer, and take every shortfall of that check as their capacity verdict; neither tells a line short only with the buffer from one short on the stock. The dashboard's edit (`updateReservation`, `apps/web/app/(dashboard)/dashboard/reservations/actions.ts:2859`–`:2886`) leaves the buffer out only when it carries `overrideTurnoverBuffer`; otherwise, whenever the edit moves what a reservation that holds stock asks for, it records `overbooked: true` with those shortfalls, and refuses only for a product the edit asks more of. So an edit that only shortens a reservation confirmed past the buffer (slice 5), or the neighbour inside its buffer, writes `overbooked: true` without any flag, and the inbox lists it, although nothing is short without the buffer. The manual creation (`createManualReservation`, `:923`–`:1077`) has no buffer flag: a booking short only with the buffer is refused with `errors.insufficientCapacity`, and sent with `allowOverbooking` it is written with `overbooked: true` and listed. Until the slice that tells the two apart (the next one), "a confirmation past the buffer lists nothing in the inbox" holds for the confirmation's own row only.
- **Outstanding payment sessions and extension checkouts after an edit.** An edit can change the balance while an older Stripe session can still be paid, or while an extension checkout is live. A late payment, the changed balance and a stale extension plan are not handled. Until this is decided, slice 12's preview warns when a payment session or an extension checkout is outstanding.

### 1. One lock function

**Done.** Every status change takes its locks through one owner entry, in the lock order, at READ COMMITTED, with one bounded restart rule. Nothing else changed but lock order, isolation and retries.

**Defect, before it.** Three paths locked the reservation in the helper, then the products, and retried a lost deadlock (`retryTransactionOnDeadlock`): the customer's acceptance, the paid confirmation (its already-confirmed repair branch included) and the marketplace's cancellation when it restored consumables. Only the store's change retried on a product added meanwhile. The failed checkout, the abandoned checkout, the marketplace's expiry and the checkout itself ran at the connection's default isolation, with no retry. No test failed when a new path inverted the order.

**The entries.** They live in `packages/db`, with the token types and the restart signal (`ReservationProductsChangedError` moved there from `packages/api`). `packages/api` and `apps/web` call them; no `packages/db → packages/api` dependency was added.

- **The owner entry**, `runReservationTransaction(work)` (`packages/db/src/reservation-change.ts:78`). It opens the transaction itself at READ COMMITTED and gives `work` an owner token, which only it creates and which ends with its transaction, committed or rolled back. On a lost deadlock, `ReservationProductsChangedError`, or a status changed under `runReservationChangeByStatus`, it rolls back and runs the whole transaction again, three runs in all, then throws `ReservationChangeBusyError`. `runReservationChange(scope, work)` (`:111`) is that entry with the products' lock taken first; `runReservationOnlyChange(scope, work)` (`:125`) with the reservation-only lock. `runReservationChangeByStatus(scope, needsProducts, work)` (`:160`) reads the status plainly, takes the products' lock when `needsProducts(status)`, the reservation's alone otherwise, and gives `work` the status read under the lock; when the reservation-only lock finds another status than the one read, the run starts over from the read, within the same three runs.
- **Which lock.** A change that moves stock or runs a stock check takes the products' lock; a change that moves none takes the reservation's alone, as it took no product lock before this slice. The products' lock: the customer's acceptance, the paid confirmation of a `pending` or `confirmed` reservation, the marketplace's cancellation of a `confirmed` or `ongoing` booking, and the store's change, which took them for every change before this slice and still does. The reservation alone: the customer's decline and cancellation, the paid confirmation of any other status (a no-op), the marketplace's cancellation of a `pending` booking, its expiry and failed payment, the abandoned and the failed checkout, the checkout replacing its pending booking. Taking the products to cancel a `pending` marketplace booking would deadlock it against a hold retry: the retry holds the attempt row while its checkout's own transaction waits for the products, a wait in JavaScript that MySQL cannot see, which ends in a lock wait timeout that nothing retries.
- **The transaction-level entries**, `lockReservationInTransaction(owner, scope)` and `lockReservationOnlyInTransaction(owner, scope)` (`packages/db/src/reservation-lock.ts:228`, `:256`), for a transaction that composes a change with other writes: they take the owner token, so they cannot run in a transaction the owner entry did not open. The first locks the reservation's products by id, then the reservation, then reads the product set again and throws the restart signal when a product joined, which reaches its owner. The second locks the reservation alone.
- **The tokens** (`ReservationLock`, `reservation-lock.ts:95`). The module records, when it issues a token, its transaction, its store, its reservation and its kind, and every check reads that record (`assertReservationLock`, `:299`), never the token's own fields; an issued token is frozen too, so its fields cannot be rewritten. A token made by hand, copied or wrapped is not one the module issued, and is refused. A class with a private member makes them nominal: no object literal type-checks as one. A `products` token allows any status change. A `reservation` token allows only a change that moves no stock and runs no `beforeWrite`: `changeReservationStatus` throws `ReservationLockError` otherwise, before the card gate and before anything is written (`packages/db/src/reservation-status.ts:312`). `restoreReservationStock` takes a `products` token, `consumeReservationStock` a `products` token or the `inserted` token of `insertReservation`.
- **What a token guarantees at run time, and what it does not.** A token of the owner entry (`products`, `reservation`, and an `inserted` token issued in its transaction, as the checkout's) is refused once its transaction ends, committed or rolled back, and in any other transaction. An `inserted` token issued in a transaction the owner entry did not open (the dashboard's creation, `apps/web/app/(dashboard)/dashboard/reservations/actions.ts:956`, whose transaction, isolation and retry this slice leaves as they were) cannot be closed by the entry: nothing tells the module when that transaction ends, so it is valid there by convention only, and its one use is the stock taken right after the lines are inserted (`:1084`). Every `inserted` token allows that one use, once: `consumeReservationStock` for its own reservation, then it is spent, so a token kept past it allows nothing. The registry guards against a caller's mistake, not against code running in the process: a token proves what the module issued, not that the caller still holds the rows it locked.

### 2. A confirmation records the verdict it checked

**Done.** Every confirmation that checks the stock records the verdict of that check, and the inbox reads it as it reads a creation's or an edit's.

**Defect, before it.** `CAPACITY_VERDICT_ACTIVITY_TYPES` was `created` and `modified`. An overbooking flagged at creation stayed in the inbox's `overbooking` source, even after a confirmation that fit the stock. It left only when an edit wrote a new verdict, the reservation ended, or it was cancelled: a quote created over the stock, whose stock was then freed and which was then confirmed, stayed in the inbox until it ended.

**The rule.**

- Every confirmation that runs a stock check writes on its own activity row the verdict of the exact demand it checked under its locks: the store's change, the customer's acceptance and the paid confirmation, all through the check of section 1 (`checkExistingReservationInventory` then; since slice 4, `checkConfirmationStock` behind the store's entry and the customer's and payment's). Its lines are read in a fixed order, by creation date then id, since the allocation places them in turn.
- It runs the check with `onShortfall: "collect"`, then refuses as before when short (until slice 4). So a confirmation writes `overbooked: false` today, and nothing else; a confirmation of a reservation never overbooked writes it too, and shows nothing (the timeline marks only `overbooked: true`).
- An observed verdict is not a store approval: `overbooked: false` means the stock fit, and nothing more. The comment where the verdict is written says so (`reservation-inventory.ts:756`).
- Once slice 4 lands, a confirmation that overrides writes `overbooked: true` with the shortfalls it booked over.
- The verdict types are `created`, `modified`, `confirmed` and `quote_accepted` (`CAPACITY_VERDICT_ACTIVITY_TYPES`, `packages/api/src/contracts/reservations.ts:844`, where the dashboard can read them too). The latest verdict wins, as before; a row without `overbooked` (a confirmation before this slice, an edit that moved no demand) is not a verdict.
- The row is dated after every earlier row of the reservation (`nextCapacityVerdictDate`, read under the reservation's lock). `changeReservationStatus` takes that date from its caller for the activity row only: `beforeWrite` returns it as `activityAt`, next to `activityMetadata`, and the status's own dates stay the change's (`packages/db/src/reservation-status.ts:354`). One row is written, as before. The store's change runs its check before its confirmation step and hands the result to `beforeWrite`.

### 4. The store may confirm over its returnable stock

**Done.** The store's confirmation books over its returnable stock when the store asks for it, and records it; the customer's acceptance, the paid confirmation and the marketplace keep refusing.

**Defect, before it.** `checkExistingReservationInventory` had no override, and `changeStoreReservationStatus` passed none. As long as the shortfall lasted, two reservations were refused at confirmation by everyone, against S3, with "no longer available" and no detail (`RESERVATION_ITEMS_UNAVAILABLE`):

- a quote created with `allowOverbooking`;
- a paid request that lost its stock.

**The rule.** The store's confirmation computes two sets of shortfalls under its locks, over the lines in the fixed order of slice 2 (`checkConfirmationStock`, `reservation-inventory.ts:751`):

- **Capacity shortfalls**: the lines short in the check without the buffer, with what that check found free. Without `allowOverbooking: true`, they refuse with `RESERVATION_INSUFFICIENT_CAPACITY` (`CONFLICT`, not retryable, `i18nKey` `errors.insufficientCapacity`; `details.shortfalls`, the `CapacityShortfall` shape of the creation and the edit: product, its name, combination, requested, available; a confirmation's also name the line, `reservationItemId`, and what it asks of the product's attributes, `selectedAttributes`, which a creation's and an edit's do not carry yet). With it, the change is made, and its activity row records `overbooked: true`, `overbookingShortfalls` and the credential (`{ kind, id }`), through `capacityVerdictMetadata`; the inbox shows it as a forced creation or edit, and the reservation's history marks the row "overbooked". A later confirmation that still finds it short writes `overbooked: true` again.
- **Buffer-only shortfalls**: the lines short in the check with the buffer and not in the one without, with what the buffered check found free. `allowOverbooking` does not cover them: they refuse with `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` (`i18nKey` `errors.turnoverBufferShortfall`, `details.shortfalls`), whatever that flag; slice 5 gives the store its own flag for them (`overrideTurnoverBuffer`). They refuse before the capacity shortfalls, so the dashboard offers to confirm anyway only what the flag can pass. A line short in both is a capacity shortfall: the store books over it whole.

**The units the reservation holds.** The override covers only the units a line does not hold yet. Whether a held unit is owed to another booking is decided apart from the allocation of the lines, against the other bookings and the reservation's held units only (`heldUnitsOwed`, `services/stock-allocation.ts`), in the check without the buffer: every unit each line holds, however many the line asks for, is taken in the order of the lines, then of their units, as an exact demand of one unit on its own combination; it is accepted when the other bookings, the held units accepted before it and itself fit at every instant (the rule of S1), and owed otherwise. What the lines ask beyond their units plays no part, so the reservation's own unassigned demand never makes one of its units owed: it is a capacity shortfall, which the flag books over. `reserveInventory` names the owed units (`uncoveredHeldUnits`). Such a unit is one no other booking holds, but that the other bookings need: one that left a choice open (a size, a colour), or one not given its units yet. It refuses with `RESERVATION_UNITS_UNAVAILABLE`, whatever the flag, after a unit's hard conflict and before the buffer and capacity refusals; its conflict carries `reason: "owed_to_other_bookings"`, which a hard conflict does not, and the message says which cause applies. The store removes the unit from the line or assigns another, then confirms again: only then does the flag book over what is still short. A held unit that fits stays held, and the flag books over the rest of its line.

The allocation itself (`allocateStock`) is untouched by this slice: it is called as on `rental-core`, so every line is served what it was, on the combination it was. What changes is that a confirmation also refuses, for every caller, a held unit the other bookings need. Against `rental-core`, that adds two refusals: a unit held by a line with an exact combination that the other bookings need (before, its line was refused only when its own combination was short), and a unit held past what its line asks for that they need (before, it was not looked at). The customer and the payment read either as stock that is gone, as for any unit conflict.

`heldUnitsOwed` also tells apart a held unit that fits nowhere even with no other booking (`unplaceable`: its combination is unknown or holds no unit, or more units are held on it than it has). That cannot happen at a confirmation: each held unit passed is a rentable unit of the product, counted in its own combination's capacity, and a unit is held by one line of a reservation at most (one assignment per line, and the assignment and the move from the product page both refuse a unit a sibling line holds). Only its unit test covers it; "owed to other bookings" is never said of such a unit.

A unit's hard conflict (`RESERVATION_UNITS_UNAVAILABLE`) and consumable stock below zero (`RESERVATION_ITEMS_UNAVAILABLE`, `errors.consumableInsufficientStock`) still refuse, whatever the flag. `allowOverbooking` is ignored on any change that takes no stock.

**Who may pass it.** The per-actor restriction is in the signatures, not a convention:

- `checkStoreConfirmationInventory(tx, id, storeId, { allowOverbooking, overrideTurnoverBuffer })` is the store's entry (slice 5 added the second option; both are required), and only `services/reservation-status.ts` calls it: `scripts/reservation-writes.test.mjs` (R7) fails on a call anywhere else.
- `checkExistingReservationInventory(tx, id, storeId)` is the customer's acceptance's and the paid confirmation's: it has no place for an override, a fourth argument is a type error (`status-change-races.db.test.ts`, `@ts-expect-error`) and is ignored at run time. Their refusals are unchanged: the customer reads `errors.productNoLongerAvailable` with no line and no unit (D1), the payment leaves the request `pending`. The marketplace confirms nothing itself: its booking is confirmed by the paid confirmation.
- A store session passes the flag from the dashboard's dialog. An API key or an agent passes it on `reservations.updateStatus` (MCP `reservations_update_status`) and is always asked for confirmation (D2): the router asks with `always` whenever the preview carries `overbooking` (`routers/dashboard/reservation-follow-up.ts:99`), even on a connection whose confirmations are off. The in-app assistant's tool does not send the flag: it calls with the user's session, which no confirmation stops, so it tells the user to confirm from the reservation's page.

**The confirmation's binding.** A confirmation with the flag previews `overbooking: { shortfalls }`, recomputed under the locks on every call, and its summary names each shortfall (none when the stock covers it). The token binds the preview's state, so a shortage that grew between the preview and the token voids the token with `CONFIRMATION_INVALID`; the next call without a token answers a new preview with the shortfalls as they are. Each shortfall names its line (`reservationItemId`) and what the line asks of the product's attributes as the stock check read it (`selectedAttributes`: the line's choices on the attributes the product has today, never its stored ones as they are), so a shortage that moved to another line of the same product, by as much, voids the token too, and so does a product that lost an attribute the line had chosen, which widens what the line asks; the summary names the choice of a line that has one ("Kayak (size: S), 2 asked, 1 free").

**Data.** None new. The input of `reservations.updateStatus` gains `allowOverbooking` (optional, default false). `CapacityShortfall` moved to `contracts/reservations.ts`, as the errors now carry it, and so did `CAPACITY_VERDICT_ACTIVITY_TYPES`, for the dashboard. A `ReservationUnitConflict` gains an optional `reason`.

**Dashboard.** Every place the store confirms (the reservations list and the product page's reservations, through `useReservationActions`; the reservation page's actions and their mobile bar, through `SmartReservationActions`) reads the refusal with `readStatusStockRefusal` (`apps/web/lib/reservations/status-stock-refusal.ts`). A capacity refusal opens the overbooking dialog of the creation and the edit (`intent: "confirm"`), whose "confirm anyway" fires `dashboard_reservation_overbooking_confirmed` with `source: "reservation_confirmation"` and sends the same change with the flag. A buffer-only refusal showed its own message and no "confirm anyway" until slice 5, which opens the same dialog for it in its own wording; the message says the buffer stops some lines, not that the stock covers the reservation, since another line may be short too. A unit refusal names the units (`errors.assignedUnitsConflict`). ### 5. The store may confirm past the turnover buffer

**Done.** The store's confirmation passes the lines that fit the stock but not the turnover buffer when the store asks for it, and records it on that confirmation alone; the customer's acceptance, the paid confirmation and the marketplace keep refusing (D5).

**Defect, before it.** After slice 4, a store confirmation whose only shortage came from the turnover buffer was refused with `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` whatever the flags: `allowOverbooking` does not cover the buffer, and nothing else did. The store could only free stock around the dates or move them, against the first standing rule, while its assignment, its move to another unit and its date edit already passed the buffer with `overrideTurnoverBuffer`.

**The rule.** The check is slice 4's, under the same locks: the lines are checked with the buffer and without it (`checkConfirmationStock`), and `allocateStock` and `heldUnitsOwed` are untouched. The override only changes which of the two verdicts refuses. The order of the refusals stays:

1. a unit's hard conflict (`RESERVATION_UNITS_UNAVAILABLE`), whatever the flags;
2. a held unit owed to other bookings (`RESERVATION_UNITS_UNAVAILABLE`, `reason: "owed_to_other_bookings"`), whatever the flags;
3. buffer-only shortfalls, the lines short with the buffer and not without it: `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` with `details.shortfalls`, counted with the buffer, unless the call carries `overrideTurnoverBuffer`;
4. capacity shortfalls: `RESERVATION_INSUFFICIENT_CAPACITY` unless it carries `allowOverbooking`.

Consumable stock below zero refuses whatever the flags (`RESERVATION_ITEMS_UNAVAILABLE`). The two flags are independent: neither covers the other's shortfalls, and a reservation short both ways needs both. A line short in both checks is a capacity shortfall (slice 4). `overrideTurnoverBuffer` is ignored on a change that takes no stock.

| Shortfalls under the locks                       | No flag                                | `allowOverbooking`                     | `overrideTurnoverBuffer`            | Both                              |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| none                                             | confirmed                              | confirmed                              | confirmed; buffer verdict `false`   | confirmed; buffer verdict `false` |
| buffer-only                                      | `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` | `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` | confirmed past the buffer           | confirmed past the buffer         |
| capacity                                         | `RESERVATION_INSUFFICIENT_CAPACITY`    | confirmed over the stock               | `RESERVATION_INSUFFICIENT_CAPACITY` | confirmed over the stock          |
| buffer-only on one line, capacity on another     | `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` | `RESERVATION_TURNOVER_BUFFER_CONFLICT` | `RESERVATION_INSUFFICIENT_CAPACITY` | confirmed, both verdicts recorded |
| a unit's hard conflict, an owed unit, consumable | refused                                | refused                                | refused                             | refused                           |

**The trace.** The confirmation's activity row records, next to the capacity verdict and through its sibling in `services/capacity-verdict.ts` (`turnoverBufferVerdictMetadata`): `turnoverBufferOverridden: true`, `turnoverBufferShortfalls` (the lines it passed, as the refusal lists them), `turnoverBufferMinutes` (the store's buffer they were checked with), and the credential (`{ kind, id }`, one key for both overrides). With the flag and no buffer-only shortfall, it records `turnoverBufferOverridden: false` and no credential for it, as slice 4 does for `overbooked: false`. Without the flag, the row says nothing of the buffer. No authority is ever read back from this row: a later confirmation checks the buffer again (D5).

**Inbox.** None from the confirmation. The store chose a short turnaround knowingly, and nothing is left to do. The `overbooking` source reads the capacity verdict only, which a buffer override leaves at `overbooked: false` on the confirmation's row. That holds for that row only: a later edit of the reservation, or of its neighbour, still records the buffer's shortfalls as `overbooked: true` and lists it (section 5).

**Who may pass it.** As `allowOverbooking` (slice 4), and held the same way:

- `checkStoreConfirmationInventory(tx, id, storeId, { allowOverbooking, overrideTurnoverBuffer })` is the store's entry; R7 keeps it to `services/reservation-status.ts`, and fails on a call elsewhere with either flag.
- `checkExistingReservationInventory(tx, id, storeId)` takes no override: a fourth argument is a type error (`@ts-expect-error` in `status-change-races.db.test.ts`). The customer's acceptance answers `errors.productNoLongerAvailable` with no line named, and the paid confirmation leaves the request `pending`, as on `rental-core`. The marketplace and the system confirm nothing themselves.
- A store session passes the flag from the dashboard's dialog. An API key or an agent passes it on `reservations.updateStatus` (MCP `reservations_update_status`) and is always asked for confirmation, with D2's mechanism: the router asks with `always` whenever the preview carries `turnoverBuffer` or `overbooking`, even on a connection whose confirmations are off. The in-app assistant's tool sends neither flag.

**The confirmation's binding.** A confirmation with the flag previews `turnoverBuffer: { shortfalls, turnoverBufferMinutes }`, recomputed under the locks on every call, and its summary names each line passed (none when the buffer stops none). The token binds the preview's state: a buffer shortfall that appeared, grew or changed, moved to another line, or another buffer setting, voids the token with `CONFIRMATION_INVALID`. With both flags, one preview carries `overbooking` and `turnoverBuffer`, and one token covers both.

**Concurrency.** Two confirmations with the flag racing for the same buffer margin of the last unit: the product's lock orders them. The first has only the buffer against it and is confirmed past it. The second then finds the unit taken without the buffer too: a capacity shortfall, which `overrideTurnoverBuffer` never passes. It is refused with `RESERVATION_INSUFFICIENT_CAPACITY` and stays as it was.

**Data.** None new, no migration. The input of `reservations.updateStatus` gains `overrideTurnoverBuffer` (optional, default false), the name the assignment and the edit already use; the preview gains `turnoverBuffer`. `CapacityShortfall` gains two optional fields, `reservationItemId` and `selectedAttributes`, which the store's confirmation fills in; the stock check hands it the choice it read of each short line (`shortfallChoices`).

**Dashboard.** Every place the store confirms (the reservations list and the product page's reservations, through `useReservationActions`; the reservation page's actions and their mobile bar, through `SmartReservationActions`) reads either stock refusal with `readStatusStockRefusal` and opens the overbooking dialog: `intent: "confirm"` for the stock, `intent: "confirmPastBuffer"` for the buffer, with its own title, text and shortfall line ("free with the buffer"). A reservation short both ways goes through two dialogs, the buffer's then the stock's, the second keeping the first's flag. The history marks a confirmation past the buffer with its own badge, "short turnaround" (`showsTurnoverBufferOverriddenBadge`, next to `showsOverbookedBadge`). The buffer refusal's message now names this choice next to freeing stock and moving the dates.

### 6b. Durable messages

**Defect.** Several paths send from memory after the commit, so a crash between the commit and the send loses the message:

- `acceptQuote`: the store's notification and `customer_quote_accepted` (`.../actions.ts:282`, `:331`);
- `declineQuote` and `cancelReservationRequest`: the store's notification (`:449`, `:582`);
- the paid checkout: its messages (section 1).

**Proposed rule.** Every status writer of this lot queues its messages as outbox rows in its transaction, as the store's path does.

The payload **freezes**, from what was read under the locks:

- the kind, the actor, and the transition with the reservation's revision;
- the customer's recipients and the language (the store's destinations are read at send time, below);
- the currency, the dates, the lines, and the tax and money facts;
- the previous period, for a modification;
- references to the documents to attach, such as the invoice by id. The sender attaches the stored document and never generates it again.

**Read at send time:**

- where a store notification goes (the owner's phone, the webhook, the members' devices), and whether the store still wants it;
- the store's customer notification settings;
- the contract link's access token, minted by the sender;
- templates.

**New effect kinds**, since `reservation.storeNotification` has no e-mail channel, no `quote_declined` event and no payment amounts (`packages/api/src/platform/effects.ts:140`):

- the store's e-mail notification;
- quote declined;
- payment received, with its amount and currency;
- `customer_quote_accepted` and `customer_reservation_confirmed` in `reservation.customerMessage`.

**Ordering and supersession.** An explicit transition × message matrix (`SUPERSEDED_CUSTOMER_MESSAGES`, extended) says which unsent customer status messages a later change makes false; only rows `pending` or `failed` are superseded, a claimed message finishes before the next of its key. A payment-received message is never superseded by a status change. Today the matrix holds the first column only (`packages/api/src/services/reservation-effects.ts:81`), applied by the store's path (`packages/api/src/services/reservation-status.ts:554`) through `supersedeEffects` (`packages/api/src/platform/effects.ts:382`).

| Later change                          | `customer_request_accepted` | `customer_quote_accepted` (new) | `customer_reservation_confirmed` (new) |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| → `cancelled`                         | superseded (today)          | superseded                      | superseded                             |
| → `rejected`                          | superseded (today)          | — (cannot precede it)           | — (cannot precede it)                  |
| → `declined`                          | — (cannot precede it)       | — (cannot precede it)           | — (cannot precede it)                  |
| → `confirmed`, `ongoing`, `completed` | kept                        | kept                            | kept                                   |

"Cannot precede it" follows the transition graph: `rejected` comes only from `pending` and `declined` only from `quote`, so neither follows an acceptance or a confirmation. If a quote is accepted and then cancelled before dispatch, no acceptance is sent. Payment received belongs to the durable payment claim: it is queued in the claim's commit, and it goes out even when the status confirmation then fails. Delivery stays at least once.

The new booking's messages belong to the creation lot.

**Test.** For each path:

- a forced error after the change rolls the transaction back and leaves no row;
- a commit leaves one row per channel, and its payload states the reservation's currency;
- a dispatch failure leaves the row for the cron;
- accept then cancel before dispatch sends the cancellation only;
- a payment whose confirmation is refused still sends payment received.

### 13. Storefront availability on the allocator

**Defect.** `getStorefrontAvailability` gives each combination `min(units of the combination − reserved on it, units of the product − reserved on the product)` (`packages/api/src/services/availability.ts:783`). Another booking that left a size open counts on the product, not on the sizes that can serve it. So a size it needs can show free, and the checkout refuses it. Four other places count the same way, per product, and the filter sums the combinations' figures (`apps/web/lib/storefront/catalog.queries.ts:514`):

- the marketplace (`marketplace-availability.ts:596`);
- the MCP calendar (`packages/mcp/src/tools/calendar.ts:110`);
- the assistant (`apps/web/lib/ai/tools.ts:1036`);
- the catalogue's filter.

**Proposed rule.**

- **Per combination.** A combination shows free the largest `q` such that a checkout line `{ candidates: [combination], quantity: q, single: true }` fits next to the other bookings at every instant. The per-combination maxima are alternatives, not quantities that add up. With two units A and B and one open booking, A shows 1 and B shows 1, and only one unit is free.
- **Per product and per filter.** The quantity for a product, or for a filter over several combinations, comes from its own allocator query, a line whose candidates are the matching combinations. It never comes from a sum.
- **Required accessories** remain a joint constraint on the parent's figure (`availability.ts:835`).
- **"Available only" filtering.** Exact filtering and counts before pagination need every candidate considered, as the catalogue does today (`catalog.queries.ts:812`). An exact filtered total is never claimed from the visible cards alone.

**What it takes.** The display loads what the write loads (`reserveInventory`), and shares that loading with it instead of copying it (ADR 0006). Prepared demands are reused across products and windows.

**Before choosing the implementation, measure:**

- a large listing;
- a multi-window calendar;
- with an upper bound on queries per request, and a latency bound.

**Test** (`packages/api/src/services/availability.db.test.ts`):

- A booking that chose size M alone, with two colours of M and one other size: the M colour it needs shows taken, and the other size shows free.
- The two-unit example above shows a product quantity of 1.
- For each combination, the quantity shown is accepted by the checkout, and one more is refused.
